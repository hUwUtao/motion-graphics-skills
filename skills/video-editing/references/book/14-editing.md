## 14. Editing

### 14.1 Core Cut Taxonomy (Hard, Match, J-Cut, L-Cut, Smash, Jump, Cross, Cutaway, Insert, Invisible, Cut on Action)

Definition: The structural and temporal juxtaposition of sequential shots across video and audio channels to control narrative continuity, kinetic momentum, and cognitive synthesis ([Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29); [Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)).
Why:

- Emotion: Split edits (`J-Cut` / `L-Cut`) mirror natural human attention—hearing a sound and turning to look, or watching a listener's face absorb the emotional weight of a speaker's final words ([Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29)).
- Attention: `Cut on Action` and `Match Cut` hide the mechanical seam of the edit inside an active visual velocity vector so viewer immersion never breaks.
- Production value: Avoiding Simultaneous "Butt Cuts" (where video, dialogue, and music all cut on the exact same frame across an entire sequence) immediately elevates editorial polish.
- Clarity: `Inserts` and `Cutaways` supply essential causal details and reaction context without breaking spatial orientation.
- Cost: Precise editorial cut timing in the sequence manifest achieves dramatic compression and continuity with zero extra rendering overhead.
  Rules:

1. Enforce the **Canonical Editorial Cut Specification**:

| Cut Type                             | Audio-Video Offset & Frame Math (@ `24 fps`)                                                                                                                    | Primary Narrative & Visual Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| :----------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hard Cut**                         | `Δt_audio = 0f` (or music continuous)                                                                                                                           | Instantaneous 0-frame switch; place cut on the speaker's blink, inhalation, or thought-change beat ([Murch](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29)).                                                                                         |
| **Cut on Action**                    | Cut **`2–3 frames` BEFORE** the peak velocity or impact in Shot A to the exact matching pose in Shot B (`-1f` overlap helps compensate for retinal persistence) | Because the eye takes `~80–120 ms` (`2–3 frames`) to re-orient across a cut, cutting slightly before the impact apex ensures the impact lands cleanly in Shot B ([Mascelli](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)). |
| **Match Cut (Graphic / Motion)**     | `0f` cut; focal center $\Delta \text{pos} \le 3\% W$, scale $\Delta S \le 10\%$, velocity vector $\Delta \theta \le 15^\circ$                                   | Bridges two completely different times, worlds, or objects via identical screen-space geometry or motion trajectory.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **J-Cut (Audio Lead)**               | Incoming Audio B starts **`6–18 frames` (`0.25–0.75 s`) BEFORE** Incoming Video B                                                                               | Propels the edit forward: the viewer hears the incoming environment or speaker first, motivating the eye to cut to the new source.                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **L-Cut (Video Lead / Audio Trail)** | Outgoing Audio A continues **`12–36 frames` (`0.5–1.5 s`) AFTER** cutting to Incoming Video B                                                                   | Anchors emotional subtext: cuts to the listener's reaction face while the speaker's crucial final phrase resonates.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Smash Cut**                        | `0f` cut paired with an instantaneous **`≥ 12–18 LU` loudness contrast** and `ΔL ≥ 0.45` luminance/tempo inversion                                              | Maximum comedic, horrific, or structural shock (e.g., quiet whispers → roaring turbine engine; dark calm → blinding chaos).                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Jump Cut**                         | Same framing (`Δangle < 15°`, `Δscale < 15%`) jumping forward in time by `≥ 15 frames`                                                                          | Violates the `30°` rule intentionally to convey frantic urgency, time-lapse compression, or breathless vlog/social authenticity.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Cross-Cutting (Parallel)**         | Alternates between Sequence A and Sequence B; cycle period accelerates from `6–8 s` down to `0.5–1.5 s` toward convergence                                      | Builds suspense by showing two simultaneous lines of action racing toward a shared climax.                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Insert Shot**                      | `CU` / `ECU` of an object _already inside_ the master scene (`duration = 18–48 frames`, `0.75–2.0 s`)                                                           | Shows critical functional detail (a ticking timer, a button press, a text message) matched strictly to master hand orientation and lighting.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Cutaway**                          | Shot of a secondary person/environment _outside_ the main action axis (`duration = 24–60 frames`)                                                               | Provides emotional reaction context or masks a time-compression trim in the main action without triggering a Jump Cut.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Invisible Cut (Hidden Stitch)**    | Hidden inside a `100%` frame-filling foreground occluder pass (`4–8f`), dark shadow, or `180°–360°` whip blur                                                   | Creates the illusion of a single continuous unbroken camera take across separate scenes or renders.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

Event mapping: Sequence timeline splice `editorial_cut_event(from_shot_id, to_shot_id, cut_type="cut_on_action"|"j_cut"|"l_cut"|"match_cut"|"smash_cut", video_cut_frame, audio_cut_frame_offset=-12, match_anchor_uv=[0.5, 0.5])`.
Raster/bake note: For **Invisible Cuts** stitching two separate 3D scene bakes, match the camera FOV, world-space velocity vector, and exposure EV identically at `Shot_A.end_frame` and `Shot_B.start_frame`, and blend across `4 frames` behind a foreground dark occluder card or motion-blur streak.
Cultural notes: Classical Soviet Montage (Eisenstein, Vertov) treats the collision of two contrasting shots (`A + B = Concept C`) as the primary generator of meaning; Classical Hollywood Continuity Editing (Murch, Schoonmaker) hides the cut so the viewer experiences an unbroken emotional stream ([Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29)).
Failure modes: Cutting _after_ an action or impact has already finished in Shot A → the energy dies in Shot A and Shot B starts flat; cutting audio and video on the exact same frame (`0f` offset) for every dialogue line in a 2-minute video → robotic, ping-pong "tennis match" editing.
Checklist: [ ] `60%+` of narrative/VO transitions use `J-Cuts` (`6–18f` lead) or `L-Cuts` (`12–36f` trail) rather than butt cuts [ ] Action cuts placed `2–3 frames` before peak impact/velocity with `1f` action overlap [ ] Graphic Match Cuts align focal coordinates within `≤ 3%` of frame width [ ] Jump cuts are either clearly intentional stylistic beats or eliminated via `≥ 30°` change / Insert / Cutaway
Sources: [Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29); [Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F).

### 14.2 Pacing, Rhythm, Continuity, Montage, and Murch's Rule of Six

Definition: The macro-temporal modulation of Average Shot Length (ASL), rhythmic shot-duration variance, and Walter Murch's weighted six-tier priority hierarchy for evaluating every cut decision ([Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29)).
Why:

- Emotion: Accelerating cut frequency (decreasing ASL) raises physiological heart rate, urgency, and excitement; decelerating cut frequency (holding a long shot after rapid montage) creates profound emotional release and gravitas.
- Attention: Varying shot durations around a rhythmic pulse prevents habituation—uniform shot lengths (`e.g., exactly 2.0 s` every cut) put the viewer's brain on autopilot.
- Production value: Prioritizing Emotion (`51%`) and Story (`23%`) over mechanical 3D spatial continuity (`4%`) per Murch's Rule of Six prevents sterile, lifeless edits that are technically continuous but emotionally dead.
- Clarity: Matches shot duration to visual complexity (`EWS` requires `2×–3×` longer viewing time than `CU`).
- Cost: Enforcing an explicit ASL curve and complexity-to-duration formula at the animatic stage prevents rendering over-long 3D shots.
  Rules:

1. **Murch's Rule of Six Priority Hierarchy**: Whenever two editorial goals conflict at a cut point, resolve the conflict strictly in descending order of Murch's canonical weights ([Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29)):
   1. **Emotion (`51%`)**: Is the cut true to the emotion of the moment? (_Never sacrifice Criterion #1 for #2–#6_).
   2. **Story (`23%`)**: Does the cut advance the narrative argument?
   3. **Rhythm (`10%`)**: Does the cut occur at a musically and kinetically right moment in time?
   4. **Eye-Trace (`7%`)**: Does the cut respect the location and movement of the audience's focus of interest within the frame?
   5. **Planarity / 2D Screen Plane (`5%`)**: Does the cut respect the 180-degree axis and screen-direction grammar?
   6. **3D Continuity of Space (`4%`)**: Does the cut respect literal physical room geometry and prop positions?
2. **Shot Duration vs. Visual Complexity Formula**:
   Base every shot's minimum duration on its information density and shot size:
   - **Close-Up (`CU` / `ECU`, single focal node, low complexity)**: `12–48 frames` (`0.5–2.0 s` @ `24 fps`) minimum to register.
   - **Medium Shot (`MS` / `MCU`, gesture + expression)**: `36–84 frames` (`1.5–3.5 s`).
   - **Wide / Establishing Shot (`WS` / `EWS`, high spatial complexity, multiple planes)**: `72–144+ frames` (`3.0–6.0+ s`) so the viewer's eye can complete `4–8` saccadic fixations across the environment `[OPINION]`.
3. **Anti-Metronome Pacing Law (Coefficient of Variation)**:
   - Even in music-driven or montage sequences, avoid cutting on the exact same bar interval (`e.g., 48f, 48f, 48f, 48f`) for more than `3–4` consecutive shots. Maintain a **Shot Length Coefficient of Variation ($\text{CV} = \sigma\_{\text{ASL}} / \mu\_{\text{ASL}}$) of `0.25–0.60`** within each structural act by alternating short kinetic clusters (`12–24 frames`) with longer breathing holds (`60–96 frames`) ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
     Event mapping: Sequence-level pacing controller `pacing_envelope_track(act_id, target_asl_seconds, min_cv_ratio=0.30, complexity_duration_scale=true)` validating all `shot_duration` values in the timeline manifest.
     Raster/bake note: N/A — Editorial pacing and Murch's Rule of Six govern temporal shot trimming and sequence assembly independent of raster shading.
     Cultural notes: Contemporary action and commercial montage averages an ASL of `1.5–2.8 seconds` (compared to `8–12 seconds` in mid-20th-century classical cinema); contemplative arthouse and luxury brand films deliberately stretch ASL to `6–15 seconds` to project confidence and stillness `[OPINION]`.
     Failure modes: Holding an `EWS` establishing shot full of architectural detail for only `18 frames` (`0.75 s`) → the viewer perceives a flash of visual noise without decoding the location; holding a static single-icon `ECU` for `5.0 seconds` after the viewer has already decoded it in `0.6 seconds` → immediate boredom and attention drop.
     Checklist: [ ] Cut decisions audited against Murch's Rule of Six (`Emotion 51% > Story 23% > Rhythm 10% > Eye-Trace 7%`) [ ] Shot duration scaled to shot size (`CU`: `0.5–2.0 s`; `MS`: `1.5–3.5 s`; `EWS`: `3.0–6.0 s`) [ ] Shot-length Coefficient of Variation $\sigma / \mu \in [0.25, 0.60]$ (no monotonous metronome cutting > 4 shots) [ ] Climax preceded by progressive ASL compression followed by a release hold
     Sources: [Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29); [Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152).
