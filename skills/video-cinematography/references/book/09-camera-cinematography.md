## 9. Camera & Cinematography

### 9.1 Focal Length, Depth of Field, Shot Sizes (ECU to EWS), and Camera Angles

Definition: The optical parameterization of the virtual camera (35mm full-frame equivalent focal length $f$, aperture $N$, focus distance $Z_{\text{focus}}$), framing scale (Extreme Close-Up to Extreme Wide Shot), and vertical/roll orientation relative to the subject ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
Why:

- Emotion: Lens focal length and camera distance control psychological intimacy and spatial distortion; vertical angle controls perceived power dominance or vulnerability ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)).
- Attention: Shallow Depth of Field (`f/1.4–f/2.8`) eliminates background high-frequency detail, forcing 100% of visual cortex processing onto the in-focus plane.
- Production value: Using real-world cinema prime focal lengths (`24mm`, `35mm`, `50mm`, `85mm`, `135mm`) rather than arbitrary default `45°` or `60°` FOV sliders gives 3D renders the optical signature of physical cinema glass.
- Clarity: Matching shot size to information type (Wide for spatial geography, Medium for body language/interaction, Close-Up for micro-detail/emotion) ensures the viewer sees what matters.
- Cost: Extreme Close-Ups (`ECU`/`CU`) require high-resolution textures and subdivision on a single hero asset but allow aggressive frustum culling of the surrounding world.
  Rules:

1. Standardize all virtual cameras to a **Full-Frame 35mm Sensor Gate (`36 mm × 24 mm`, or `36 mm × 20.25 mm` for 16:9)** where horizontal Field of View is $\text{FOV}_H = 2 \arctan\left(\frac{36\text{ mm}}{2 f}\right)$, and select from the **Cinema Prime Focal Length Table** ([Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)):
   - **Ultra-Wide (`14–20 mm`, $\text{FOV}\_H \approx 104^\circ\text{–}84^\circ$)**: Exaggerates Z-depth, elongates z-axis motion velocity, distorts close subjects; use for monumental architectural scale, disorientation, or kinetic action.
   - **Wide / Environmental (`24–28 mm`, $\text{FOV}\_H \approx 74^\circ\text{–}65^\circ$)**: Immersive spatial presence while retaining dynamic perspective lines.
   - **Natural / Narrative Standard (`35–50 mm`, $\text{FOV}\_H \approx 54^\circ\text{–}40^\circ$)**: Matches natural human spatial perception; workhorse for `MS` and `MCU` shots.
   - **Portrait / Hero Product (`85–105 mm`, $\text{FOV}\_H \approx 24^\circ\text{–}19^\circ$)**: Flattens facial/product perspective distortion and melts backgrounds into creamy bokeh; **mandatory default for hero 3D product beauty shots** (`85–120 mm` avoid the bulbous "fish-eye" bevel distortion of wide lenses).
   - **Telephoto / Graphic Compression (`135–200+ mm` or `Orthographic`)**: Compresses Z-space so foreground and background planes appear stacked like flat 2D graphic layers; ideal for isometric/axonometric technical diagrams.
2. Standardize **Shot Sizes** by subject height relative to Frame Height ($H$) ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)):
   - **EWS (Extreme Wide / Establishing)**: Subject occupies `< 15% H`; geography dominates.
   - **WS / FS (Wide / Full Shot)**: Full subject head-to-toe occupies `75%–90% H`.
   - **MWS / Cowboy (`3/4` Shot)**: Knees/mid-thigh to head.
   - **MS (Medium Shot)**: Waist to head (`50%` of body fills `85% H`); balances gesture and expression.
   - **MCU (Medium Close-Up)**: Chest/shoulders to top of head; standard for dialogue and interviews.
   - **CU (Close-Up)**: Chin to forehead (`face/hero component fills 75%–90% H`); intimate emotional read.
   - **ECU (Extreme Close-Up / Macro)**: Single eye, watch gear, or texture detail fills `100% H`.
3. Map **Camera Vertical Pitch & Roll Angles** to psychological power dynamics ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)):
   - **Eye-Level (`0° ± 3°` pitch, `0°` roll)**: Neutral parity, documentary objectivity.
   - **Low Angle (`-15° to -45°` camera below subject looking up)**: Grants the subject dominance, heroism, or menace.
   - **High Angle (`+15° to +45°` camera above subject looking down)**: Renders the subject vulnerable, diminished, or overzicht/tactical.
   - **Bird's-Eye / Overhead (`+85° to +90°` top-down)**: Abstract geometric pattern, fate, or flat-lay tabletop diagram.
   - **Dutch Tilt (`Roll = ±8° to ±25°`)**: Psychological unease, disequilibrium, or kinetic rebellion (never use accidental `1°–3°` roll).
4. Compute **Depth of Field Circle of Confusion (CoC)** in pixels from physical optical parameters:
   $$\text{CoC}_{\text{px}} = \left|\frac{Z - Z_{\text{focus}}}{Z}\right| \cdot \frac{f^2}{N \cdot (Z_{\text{focus}} - f)} \cdot \frac{W_{\text{px}}}{W_{\text{sensor}}}$$
   When rendering macro close-ups (`Z_focus < 0.3 m`), scale the virtual sensor or stop down to `f/8–f/16` so the entire hero object doesn't dissolve into unintelligible blur (unless an intentional miniature tilt-shift effect is desired) ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
   Event mapping: `camera_state_event(shot_size="MCU", focal_length_mm=85, sensor_width_mm=36, f_stop=2.8, focus_target_id="hero_mesh", pitch_deg=-18, roll_deg=0)`.
   Raster/bake note: Implement Depth of Field in the GPU raster pipeline using a separable Gather/Scatter Tile-Max CoC pass (splitting near-field foreground bleed and far-field background defocus into separate half-resolution buffers with `32–64` polygonal/circular bokeh samples) evaluated in **Scene-Linear RGB before tone mapping** so out-of-focus specular highlights bloom into bright bokeh discs rather than dull gray circles.
   Cultural notes: Yasujirō Ozu famously anchored his camera on a low `3-foot` tatami-level tripod (`50mm` lens) across entire films; commercial hardware design films (industrial hardware showcase style) rely almost exclusively on `85mm–135mm` telephoto lenses or pure orthographic projection to preserve parallel geometric product edges `[OPINION]`.
   Failure modes: Placing a `24mm` wide lens `20 cm` from a character's face or a product box → grotesque "big-nose" / bulging perspective distortion; computing DoF blur after sRGB gamma encoding → specular bokeh discs turn into muddy gray hexagons.
   Checklist: [ ] Focal length selected from standard cinema primes (`24`, `35`, `50`, `85`, `135 mm`) on `36 mm` gate [ ] Hero product/portrait close-ups use `≥ 85 mm` focal length [ ] Shot size (`EWS` to `ECU`) and vertical pitch (`Low`, `Eye`, `High`) match narrative beat [ ] DoF CoC evaluated in scene-linear space before tone mapping
   Sources: [Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450); [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html).

### 9.2 Camera Moves (Pan, Tilt, Dolly, Truck, Crane, Orbit, Push-In, Handheld)

Definition: The kinetic choreography of the virtual camera's nodal rotation (`Pan`, `Tilt`, `Roll`) and world-space translation (`Dolly`, `Truck`, `Crane/Pedestal`, `Orbit`) over time ([Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
Why:

- Emotion: A slow `Dolly Push-In` builds intimacy, realization, or mounting dread; a rising `Crane/Pedestal Up` evokes transcendence, conclusion, or expanding scope; `Handheld` micro-shake injects visceral documentary immediacy or panic.
- Attention: Camera movement re-weights spatial salience continuously and activates motion parallax across depth planes.
- Production value: Constraining a camera rig to physically plausible crane/dolly mechanics (separating dolly track translation from fluid-head pan/tilt damping) eliminates the weightless "flying 6-DOF ghost camera" look of amateur CG ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
- Clarity: Reveals spatial relationships progressively (e.g., tracking from a cause in the foreground to an effect in the background).
- Cost: Translating the camera through a static baked 3D scene generates rich kinetic energy on every pixel without animating a single mesh.
  Rules:

1. Enforce the **Motivated Camera Move Taxonomy & Speed Limits**:

| Camera Move                      | Transform Channel                                              | Speed / Angular Rate Limit (@ `24 fps`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Psychological Effect & Execution Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| :------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pan (Horizontal Rotation)**    | Nodal Yaw (`Y-axis` rotation, zero translation)                | `< 5°–7°/s` (avoid judder) OR `> 120°/s` (whip pan); **avoid the `10°–35°/s` strobing dead zone** ([Brown](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)) | Surveys horizontal geography or follows a moving subject; produces **zero parallax**. Prefer `Truck` over `Pan` in 3D scenes whenever depth cues are desired.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Tilt (Vertical Rotation)**     | Nodal Pitch (`X-axis` rotation, zero translation)              | `< 4°–6°/s`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Reveals vertical stature or scale from base to apex.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Dolly In / Push-In**           | Local `-Z` translation toward subject                          | `2%–15%` focal-distance reduction per second (`quad_in_out` or linear drift)                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Concentrates viewer focus into the subject's internal state; physically changes foreground-to-background size ratio (unlike optical `Zoom`, which magnifies flatly).                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Dolly Out / Pull-Back**        | Local `+Z` translation away from subject                       | `5%–20%` distance increase per second                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Reveals environmental context, isolation, or surprise surroundings.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Truck (Lateral Track)**        | Local `±X` translation parallel to subject plane               | Matched to subject speed or `0.1–1.5 m/s`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Maximizes **Foreground/Background Parallax**; always include `2–4` foreground elements passing between camera and subject.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Crane / Pedestal / Jib**       | World `±Y` vertical translation + counter-tilt                 | `0.1–1.0 m/s` vertical with `cubic_in_out` damping                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Sweeping establishing opening (Crane Down into action) or emotional resolution (Crane Up and away).                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Orbit (Arc Dolly)**            | Polar azimuth $\theta(t)$ around `look_at` pivot at radius $R$ | `5°–25°/s` for beauty shots; `45°–90°/s` for heroic climax                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Sculptural 360° inspection of 3D form and specular light wrap.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Dolly Zoom (Vertigo / Zolly)** | Simultaneous `Dolly ±Z` + inverse `Focal Length f(t)`          | Lock subject screen width constant: $f(t) = f_0 \cdot \frac{Z(t)}{Z_0}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Visceral psychological shock, realization, or reality warping as the background violently expands or collapses behind a stationary subject ([Brown](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)). |
| **Handheld (Procedural Shake)**  | Additive 2-band Perlin/Simplex noise on `Pitch`, `Yaw`, `Roll` | Low breath band: `0.4–0.8 Hz` (`±0.3°–0.8°`); High tremor band: `3.5–7.0 Hz` (`±0.05°–0.25°`)                                                                                                                                                                                           | Documentary realism (`low amplitude`) or chaotic impact/explosion (`high amplitude` decaying exponentially with $\tau = 0.3\text{ s}$).                                                                                                                                                                                           |

2. **Two-Node Rig Architecture (Anti-Spline-Wobble Rule)**: Never animate a single free-floating camera's 6-DOF `(X, Y, Z, Pitch, Yaw, Roll)` on a single multi-point spline—this causes the camera to bank and swing wildly like a roller coaster. Always construct a **Parent-Child Rig**: (a) `Dolly_Base` translates smoothly along a simple 2-knot line or circular arc, and (b) `Camera_Head` aims at an explicit `Look_At_Target` node with a damped spring lag (`ζ = 0.85, ωₙ = 10–16 rad/s`) so the framing trails the subject slightly like a human camera operator on a fluid head ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
3. **Cut into Motion / Cut out of Motion**: For editorial sequences where the camera moves across multiple shots, start the camera move `4–6 frames` _before_ the incoming cut (so the shot is already in motion at `frame_in`) or hold the static frame for `≥ 18 frames` before initiating a deliberately motivated move; cutting from a moving shot to a camera that sits dead still for `3 frames` and then suddenly lurches into motion feels sloppy ([Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29)).
   Event mapping: `camera_rig_move_event(rig_id, move_type="dolly_push"|"truck"|"orbit"|"zolly", start_frame, duration_frames, dolly_delta_vec, look_at_target_id, head_damping_zeta=0.85, handheld_intensity=0.0)`.
   Raster/bake note: Output a per-pixel 2D screen-space velocity buffer (`RG16_SFLOAT` storing `uv_curr - uv_prev` from camera + object matrices) on every raster pass to drive accurate post-process camera and object motion blur.
   Cultural notes: Classical Hollywood and David Fincher's cinematography lock the camera head to the exact velocity vector of the subject with zero overshoot (clinical omniscience), whereas documentary/vérité and Bourne-style action use reactive `85mm+` handheld rigs with intentional `4–8 frame` operator reaction lag `[OPINION]`.
   Failure modes: Panning across high-contrast vertical edges at `15°–25°/s` at `24 fps` (`7–12 px/frame` displacement) → severe, headache-inducing **24p Judder / Strobe** ([ASC / Brown, Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)); animating camera translation and rotation on a 5-point Bézier spline without a Look-At target → horizon tilts accidentally and velocity pulses at every knot.
   Checklist: [ ] Camera built as a Two-Node Rig (`Dolly_Base` + damped `Look_At` head) [ ] Panning speed avoids the `7–15 px/frame` (`10°–35°/s`) 24p judder zone [ ] Lateral `Truck` or `Orbit` favored over nodal `Pan` to generate 3D parallax [ ] Handheld shake uses dual-frequency (`0.6 Hz` breath + `5 Hz` micro-tremor) rotational noise [ ] Cuts across moving cameras maintain velocity continuity at `frame_in` / `frame_out`
   Sources: [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450); [Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F); [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html); [Murch, In the Blink of an Eye](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FIn_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_%28Murch_book%29).

### 9.3 180-Degree Rule, 30-Degree Rule, Shutter Angle, Motion Blur, and Aspect Ratio

Definition: The spatial continuity laws governing multi-camera placement across cuts (`180° Line of Action` and `30° Axis Delta`), the temporal exposure integration window (`Shutter Angle` and `Motion Blur`), and the frame geometry (`Aspect Ratio`) ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
Why:

- Emotion: Respecting spatial continuity keeps the viewer emotionally inside the scene; shutter angle alters visceral texture (`180°` = natural cinema flow; `45°–90°` = staccato combat nervousness; `360°` = dreamy smeared intoxication).
- Attention: Prevents disorienting left-right screen flips across cuts and uses motion blur to fuse discrete frames into continuous trajectories.
- Production value: Un-blurred `0°` shutter 3D renders at `24–30 fps` look brittle and synthetic; cutting `< 30°` on the same subject looks like an accidental hiccup jump cut.
- Clarity: Keeps characters' eyelines and travel vectors consistent (`Subject A looks right → Subject B looks left`).
- Cost: Screen-space velocity-buffer motion blur (`16–24 taps` along velocity vector) delivers `180°` shutter smoothness in `< 0.5 ms` compared to `16×` multi-frame accumulation rendering.
  Rules:

1. **The 180-Degree Rule (Line of Action)**: Establish an invisible axis connecting two interacting subjects (or along a single subject's travel vector). Keep all camera placements on **one side (`180°` arc)** of this axis across an entire scene so Subject A always occupies the left screen half looking right and Subject B occupies the right screen half looking left ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)). Only cross the line via: (a) a continuous on-screen camera orbit/track across the axis, (b) a neutral `0°` dead-on buffer shot on the axis, or (c) an intentional psychological reversal shock.
2. **The 30-Degree Rule**: When cutting between two shots of the same subject without an intervening cutaway, change the camera's horizontal/vertical angle around the subject by **`≥ 30°`** AND/OR change the focal distance/shot size by **`≥ 35%`** (e.g., `MS` to `CU`). Camera changes `< 30°` at similar scale register on the retina as an unintentional jump-cut glitch ([Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F)).
3. **Shutter Angle & Motion Blur Integration**:
   - Define temporal exposure duration via rotary **Shutter Angle ($\theta\_{\text{shutter}} \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ]$)** where exposure fraction of a frame interval is $\Delta t_{\text{exp}} = \frac{\theta_{\text{shutter}}}{360^\circ \cdot \text{fps}}$ ([Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)):
     - **Standard Cinematic (`180°` shutter = `50%` frame interval, `1/48 s` @ `24 fps`)**: Default for `90%` of narrative, 3D, and motion graphics sequences.
     - **Crisp Graphic / Staccato Action (`72°–120°` shutter = `20%–33%` frame interval)**: Ideal for fast 2D geometric typography and high-tension kinetic impacts where excessive smear would blur letterforms.
     - **Stop-Motion / Stepped Anime (`0°–30°` shutter)**: Zero or minimal blur when animating on `2s` (`12 fps`) so crisp posed silhouettes read as intentional tactile frames.
     - **Dream / Whip Transition / Light Trails (`270°–360°` shutter = `75%–100%` frame interval)**: Maximum smear across whip pans or surreal sequences.
   - Always use **Center- on-Frame Shutter Phase** (`[-0.5 * Δt_exp, +0.5 * Δt_exp]`) so the motion blur streak is centered symmetrically on the exact event timestamp `t` (matching audio hit points).
4. **Aspect Ratio Selection**:
   - `16:9` (`1.778:1`): Universal broadcast, YouTube, desktop, and presentation standard ([ITU-R BT.709](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.itu.int%2Frec%2Fr-rec-bt.709)).
   - `2.39:1` (Anamorphic Scope): Epic horizontal staging, environmental isolation, and two-shot tension.
   - `9:16` (`0.5625:1`): Vertical mobile full-screen (TikTok, Reels, Shorts); requires vertical stacking of staging planes rather than wide horizontal two-shots.
   - `4:5` (`0.80:1`) / `1:1` (`1.00:1`): Social feed carousels and square product cards.
     Event mapping: Camera continuity validator checking `abs(cam_B.azimuth - cam_A.azimuth) >= 30.0` and `sign(cross(line_of_action, cam_A.pos)) == sign(cross(line_of_action, cam_B.pos))`, paired with `motion_blur_pass(shutter_angle_deg=180.0, phase="centered", samples=20)`.
     Raster/bake note: Implement raster motion blur using a **Tile-Max Neighbor Velocity Gather** shader (`16–24 taps` in linear RGB before tone mapping) driven by the velocity AOV (`RG16_SFLOAT`). **Quality loss vs. path-traced sub-frame accumulation**: screen-space velocity blur cannot curve around fast-spinning wheels/propellers (it blurs along a straight linear tangent vector); for fast-spinning rotors (`> 45°/frame`), swap the mesh to a pre-baked radial-blurred disc planar card.
     Cultural notes: Steven Spielberg and Janusz Kamiński used a `45°` shutter in _Saving Private Ryan_ to create hyper-sharp staccato combat chaos, while Wong Kar-wai and Christopher Doyle used step-printing with `360°` shutter (`8–12 fps`) to create romantic neon motion trails `[OPINION]`.
     Failure modes: Cutting between two shots that differ by only `10°` in angle and `10%` in scale → subject appears to twitch violently in place; rendering fast `24 fps` 3D motion with `0°` shutter (no motion blur) → painful stroboscopic jitter across every fast transition.
     Checklist: [ ] All cameras in a scene stay on the same side of the `180°` Line of Action [ ] Consecutive cuts on the same subject differ by `≥ 30°` angle or `≥ 35%` scale [ ] Shutter angle set to `180°` (cinema/3D) or `90°–120°` (crisp 2D type) with centered phase [ ] Fast-spinning wheels (`> 45°/frame`) use radial-blur cards to avoid linear velocity-vector artifacts
     Sources: [Mascelli, The Five C's of Cinematography](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.silmanjamespress.com%2Fshop%2Ffilmmaking-directing%2Fthe-five-cs-of-cinematography%2F); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450); [ITU-R BT.709](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.itu.int%2Frec%2Fr-rec-bt.709).
