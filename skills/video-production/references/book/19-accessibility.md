## 19. Accessibility

### 19.1 Captions, Luminance Contrast Ratios, and Minimum On-Screen Read Time

Definition: The normative legibility, luminance contrast, reading-rate, and timed-text synchronization rules ensuring all visual and auditory information is accessible to users with hearing or vision impairments ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG22%2F); [Netflix Timed Text Style Guide / Partner Help](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fstudiopartner.netflix.net%2Fstudio%2Fdelivery-specifications-and-requirements-external)).
Why:

- Emotion: Clean, well-timed captions that respect shot changes preserve dramatic timing and comedy punchlines for deaf, hard-of-hearing, and sound-off viewers.
- Attention: High luminance contrast (`≥ 4.5:1` to `7.0:1`) eliminates squinting and eye fatigue across mobile and bright-ambient viewing conditions.
- Production value: Captions that snap cleanly across cut boundaries without straddling a cut by `3 frames` mark professional broadcast post-production ([Netflix Partner Help](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fstudiopartner.netflix.net%2Fstudio%2Fdelivery-specifications-and-requirements-external)).
- Clarity: Guarantees `100%` text and data legibility across color-vision deficiencies (protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia) and low-vision displays.
- Cost: Enforcing WCAG contrast formulas and CPS limits programmatically in the scene validator catches legibility violations in milliseconds before rendering.
  Rules:

1. **WCAG 2.2 Luminance Contrast Thresholds ([WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fcontrast-minimum.html) / [SC 1.4.6](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG22%2F) / [SC 1.4.11](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG21%2FUnderstanding%2Fnon-text-contrast.html))**:
   - Compute relative luminance in linear sRGB ($Y = 0.2126 R_{\text{lin}} + 0.7152 G_{\text{lin}} + 0.0722 B_{\text{lin}}$ per [ITU-R BT.709](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.itu.int%2Frec%2Fr-rec-bt.709)) and contrast ratio $C_{\text{ratio}} = \frac{Y_{\text{lighter}} + 0.05}{Y_{\text{darker}} + 0.05}$:
     - **Body / Caption Text (`< 4.5% H`)**: Enforce **$C\_{\text{ratio}} \ge 4.5 : 1$** minimum (WCAG AA) and target **`≥ 7.0 : 1`** (WCAG AAA) because moving video backgrounds degrade effective legibility compared to static web pages!
     - **Large Display Headlines (`≥ 5.0% H` bold or `≥ 6.5% H` regular)**: Enforce **$C\_{\text{ratio}} \ge 3.0 : 1$** minimum (WCAG AA) and target **`≥ 4.5 : 1`**.
     - **Essential Graphical Icons, Chart Lines, & UI Boundaries ([SC 1.4.11](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG21%2FUnderstanding%2Fnon-text-contrast.html))**: Enforce **$C\_{\text{ratio}} \ge 3.0 : 1$** against adjacent background pixels.
   - When placing white text (`Y = 1.0`) over bright or variable video footage, always back the text with a **Semi-Transparent Dark Pill Box (`opacity = 65%–80%` black, corner radius `0.8% H`, padding `0.8% H`)**, an outer dark stroke (`width = 6%–10%` of font size), or a localized soft gradient scrim so $C_{\text{ratio}} \ge 7.0:1$ is guaranteed regardless of background motion.
2. **Color-Vision Deficiency (CVD) Redundant Coding Rule ([WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG22%2F))**:
   - Never rely on hue alone (e.g., Red vs. Green lines/dots) to distinguish data series or status states. Pair every hue difference with a **Lightness separation ($\Delta L \ge 0.25$ in OKLab)** AND a **Secondary Geometric Cue** (distinct dash pattern, icon shape, direct text label, or texture hatching).
3. **Timed-Text / Caption Synchronization & Reading Speed ([Netflix Partner Help](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fstudiopartner.netflix.net%2Fstudio%2Fdelivery-specifications-and-requirements-external))**:
   - **Reading Speed Ceiling**: `≤ 17 characters per second (CPS)` for adult programming (`≤ 13 CPS` for children's content).
   - **Minimum & Maximum Event Duration**: Every caption event must stay on screen for **`≥ 5/6 second` (`833 ms`, `20 frames` @ `24 fps`)** and **`≤ 7.0 seconds`**.
   - **Line Constraints**: Maximum **`2 lines`** per caption card, **`≤ 42 characters per line`**, broken at natural syntactic clause boundaries (never split a determiner/adjective from its noun across lines).
   - **Shot-Cut Alignment Gap**: Maintain a minimum **`2-frame` gap** between consecutive caption cards. If a speech phrase ends within `1–11 frames` of a visual shot cut, **snap the caption's out-point directly to the cut frame** (never let a caption hang `3 frames` over a cut into a new scene or stop `3 frames` before a cut) ([Netflix Partner Help](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fstudiopartner.netflix.net%2Fstudio%2Fdelivery-specifications-and-requirements-external)).
     Event mapping: `caption_track_validator(min_contrast_ratio=7.0, max_cps=17.0, min_duration_ms=833, max_duration_ms=7000, max_lines=2, max_chars_per_line=42, snap_to_cut_tolerance_frames=11)`.
     Raster/bake note: Sample the underlying rendered framebuffer inside each text element's bounding box via a compute reduction pass (finding `min_Y` and `max_Y` behind the glyphs) to verify $C_{\text{ratio}} \ge 4.5:1$ automatically on every rendered frame, or dynamically adjust the backing scrim opacity channel to maintain target contrast.
     Cultural notes: Closed Captions (`.srt` / `.vtt` / `CEA-708`) must include non-speech atmospheric and narrative sound descriptors in brackets (`[ominous synth pulse]`, `[glass shatters]`) for deaf and hard-of-hearing parity, whereas Open "Burned-In" Social Subtitles transcribe only spoken words.
     Failure modes: Placing pure white un-stroked text over a white shirt or bright sky in a moving shot → text vanishes completely for `2 seconds`; letting a subtitle card linger `4 frames` into the next shot after a hard cut → triggers a visual double-take that makes the cut feel broken.
     Checklist: [ ] Text luminance contrast ratio $\ge 4.5:1$ (`≥ 7.0:1` target over moving video via scrim/pill/stroke) [ ] Non-text icons and chart elements achieve $\ge 3.0:1$ contrast and use redundant non-hue cues [ ] Caption reading speed `≤ 17 CPS`, duration `0.833–7.0 s`, `≤ 2 lines`, `≤ 42 chars/line` [ ] Captions within `11 frames` of a shot cut are snapped cleanly to the cut boundary
     Sources: [WCAG 2.2 Specification](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG22%2F); [WCAG 2.2 Understanding SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fcontrast-minimum.html); [WCAG 2.1 Understanding SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG21%2FUnderstanding%2Fnon-text-contrast.html); [Netflix Partner Help Center](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fstudiopartner.netflix.net%2Fstudio%2Fdelivery-specifications-and-requirements-external).

### 19.2 Photosensitive Seizure / Flash Limits (WCAG SC 2.3.1) and Reduced Motion Alternatives

Definition: The strict photic flash frequency, luminance delta, saturated-red transition, and screen-area limits required to prevent photosensitive epileptic seizures ([WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fthree-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [ITU-R BT.1702](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG20-TECHS%2FG15)), paired with vestibular-safe reduced-motion choreography.
Why:

- Emotion: Protects viewers from physical discomfort, migraines, vertigo, and life-threatening photosensitive seizures.
- Attention: Replaces cheap, dangerous strobe-light flashing with safe localized luminance pulses (`< 25%` screen area or `< 3 Hz`).
- Production value: Passing automated Harding / WCAG 2.3.1 PSE (Photosensitive Epilepsy) broadcast QC is a mandatory legal gate for television, streaming, and game distribution globally.
- Clarity: Eliminates disorienting full-screen flicker and violent camera roll that obscure visual comprehension.
- Cost: Auditing flash frequency (`≤ 3 flashes/s`) and relative luminance delta ($\Delta Y < 0.10$) during event scheduling prevents broadcast master rejection and re-rendering.
  Rules:

1. **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1 & ITU-R BT.1702 General Flash Threshold (Hard Safety Law)**:
   - Never allow any sequence to violate all three of the **General Flash Criteria** simultaneously ([WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fthree-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)):
     1. **Frequency Limit**: More than **`3 flashes`** (6 opposing light-dark transitions) within any rolling **`1.0-second` window** (`f > 3.0 Hz`; human cortical seizure sensitivity peaks sharply in the `10–25 Hz` band, i.e., alternating every `1–2 frames` at `24–60 fps`).
     2. **Luminance Delta Limit**: A pair of opposing transitions where the relative luminance $Y \in [0.0, 1.0]$ changes by **`≥ 10%` ($\Delta Y \ge 0.10$)** AND the darker image's relative luminance is **`< 0.80`**.
     3. **Screen Area Limit**: The flashing pixels occupy a combined contiguous or proximate area exceeding **`25%` of any `10°` visual field viewport** (conservatively enforced in production as **`≤ 10%–20%` of total frame area**).
2. **The Saturated Red Flash Prohibition ([WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fthree-flashes-or-below-threshold.html))**:
   - The human retina and visual cortex are uniquely hypersensitive to flashing deep red light (`600–700 nm`), even when luminance $Y$ remains constant!
   - Define a **Saturated Red** pixel as $\frac{R_{\text{lin}}}{R_{\text{lin}} + G_{\text{lin}} + B_{\text{lin}}} \ge 0.80$, and forbid more than **`3` transitions per second** where $\Delta(R_{\text{lin}} - G_{\text{lin}} - B_{\text{lin}}) \times 320 > 20$ across `> 10%` of frame area.
3. **Safe Impact Flash Technique**:
   - When an action anime hit, lightning strike, or trailer impact requires an energetic flash, keep it 100% PSE-compliant by either: (a) executing **at most `1` or `2` flashes** within any `1.0-second` window (e.g., `1 frame` white at `35%` opacity → `6 frame` smooth exponential decay to normal, never alternating `black-white-black-white-black-white` on consecutive frames), OR (b) confining the high-contrast flash strictly to a localized rim/spark element covering **`< 10%` of total screen area**.
4. **Vestibular & Reduced-Motion Profile ([WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG22%2F))**:
   - When authoring a `reduced_motion` variant (or avoiding vestibular nausea in general audiences), cap continuous full-screen optical zoom/parallax translation to `< 20%` screen area or replace `6-frame` whip pans / Z-axis tunnel zooms with clean `12–18 frame` cross-dissolves and subtle `±3%` planar slides.
     Event mapping: Runtime safety gate `pse_flash_validator(max_flashes_per_sec=3.0, max_delta_relative_luma=0.10, max_flashing_screen_area_pct=15.0, block_saturated_red_alternation=true)`.
     Raster/bake note: Include a compute-shader temporal luminance & red-saturation histogram monitor over a rolling `1.0-second` ring buffer (`24–60 frames`) broken into an `8×8` screen grid; if any `2×2` sub-block accumulates `> 3` opposing transitions with $\Delta Y \ge 0.10$ in `1.0 s`, automatically clamp temporal luminance contrast or flag the exact timecode.
     Cultural notes: Following the 1997 Japanese broadcast incident (_Pokémon_ Episode 38, which alternated full-screen saturated red and blue at `12 Hz` and hospitalized 685 viewers), Japan's NAB/NHK and the UK's Ofcom (Harding Test) enacted strict automated PSE rejection gates now universally enforced by Netflix, YouTube, and broadcast networks ([WCAG G15 / ITU-R BT.1702](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG20-TECHS%2FG15)).
     Failure modes: Animating a strobe effect by toggling background visibility `ON / OFF` every `2 frames` at `24 fps` (`6 flashes/second` at `100%` screen area, $\Delta Y = 1.0$) → catastrophic WCAG 2.3.1 violation and severe seizure hazard; alternating full-screen saturated Red and Blue cards rapidly → triggers red-flash seizure hazard even at equal luminance.
     Checklist: [ ] Zero sequences exceed `3 flashes/second` with $\Delta Y \ge 0.10$ across `> 10%–25%` of frame area [ ] Zero rapid alternations involving Saturated Red ($R / (R+G+B) \ge 0.80$) [ ] Impact flashes use a single decayed pulse (`1f` peak + `6f` exponential tail) rather than multi-frame strobing [ ] Camera roll and violent Z-axis parallax bounded to prevent vestibular motion sickness
     Sources: [WCAG 2.2 Understanding SC 2.3.1: Three Flashes or Below Threshold](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fthree-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [W3C Technique G15 / ITU-R BT.1702](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FTR%2FWCAG20-TECHS%2FG15); [WCAG 2.2 Understanding SC 2.3.2 Three Flashes](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fthree-flashes.html).
