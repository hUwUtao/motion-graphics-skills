## 4. Color

### 4.1 Perceptual Color Spaces (OKLab, OKLCH, CIELAB) vs. Display Spaces

Definition: The mathematical parameterization of color for authoring, palette generation, and interpolation using perceptually uniform coordinates—specifically Lightness ($L$), Chroma ($C$), and Hue ($h$) in [OKLCH](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F)—rather than display-encoded sRGB, HSV, or HSL.
Why:

- Emotion: Preserves intended mood and luminance weight when shifting hues across scenes without accidental brightness spikes.
- Attention: Prevents high-luminance hues in non-perceptual spaces (such as sRGB yellow having `Y = 0.928` vs. sRGB blue having `Y = 0.072`) from hijacking visual salience when equal visual weight is intended.
- Production value: Eliminates the muddy gray dead-zones and neon cyan/yellow spikes that plague naive sRGB and HSL color ramps.
- Clarity: Guarantees predictable lightness contrast ($\Delta L$) for legibility across any hue angle.
- Cost: Computing palettes and gradients in OKLab/OKLCH requires only a $3\times 3$ linear matrix + cube-root nonlinearity + $3\times 3$ matrix per [Ottosson (2020)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F), adding negligible shader or authoring cost.
  Rules:

1. Author all color palettes, procedural color variations, and UI/graphic lightness ramps in **OKLCH** ($L \in [0.0, 1.0]$, $C \in [0.0, 0.37]$, $h \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ)$) rather than HSL or HSV ([Ottosson, Oklab](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F)).
2. Prefer **OKLab / OKLCH** over **CIELAB / CIELCh** ([CIE 1976 Lab\*](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FCIELAB_color_space)) for hue interpolation and saturation scaling: CIELAB exhibits a documented perceptual hue-linearity warp in the blue region ($h \approx 270^\circ\text{–}300^\circ$) where desaturating or blending blue shifts purple/magenta, whereas OKLab maintains constant perceived hue across chroma changes ([Ottosson, Oklab](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F)).
3. When equal visual prominence is required across multiple categorical elements (e.g., data visualization bars, multi-icon sets, secondary graphic shapes), lock $L$ constant within $\pm 0.02$ and $C$ constant within $\pm 0.015$ in OKLCH while varying only $h$ ([Ottosson, Oklab](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F)).
4. Perform gamut mapping from unbounded OKLCH to the target display gamut (sRGB / BT.709 or Display P3) by **reducing Chroma $C$ at constant Lightness $L$ and constant Hue $h$** (binary search along $C$) rather than naïve RGB channel clipping, which corrupts hue and lightness ([Ottosson, Okhsv and Okhsl](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Fcolorpicker%2F)).
   Event mapping: Color-channel events (`color_event`) parameterized in `oklch(L, C, h, alpha)` with interpolation mode set to `space="oklab"` (Cartesian path) or `space="oklch_shorter"` (cylindrical path), converted to linear scene RGB prior to shader lighting evaluation.
   Raster/bake note: Store texture assets in linear-decoded RGB (`VK_FORMAT_R8G8B8A8_SRGB` or `R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT`), execute lighting in linear RGB, and execute procedural color ramps/mixes inside fragment shaders using the analytic OKLab matrix transform.
   Cultural notes: Digital design systems (CSS Color Module Level 4, modern UI/motion frameworks) have standardized on `oklch()` and `oklab()` to replace legacy `hsl()` workflows across web and broadcast graphics.
   Failure modes: Rotating hue in HSL (`hsl(h, 100%, 50%)`) across a sequence → perceived brightness swings wildly by `> 4.5:1` luminance ratio as hue passes through yellow (`L_oklab ≈ 0.97`) vs. blue (`L_oklab ≈ 0.45`); clipping out-of-gamut OKLCH colors via `clamp(rgb, 0, 1)` → saturated blues turn cyan or purple.
   Checklist: [ ] Palettes authored in OKLCH rather than HSL/HSV [ ] Categorical hues share equal $L$ ($\pm 0.02$) and $C$ ($\pm 0.015$) [ ] Out-of-gamut colors mapped by reducing $C$ at constant $L$ and $h$ [ ] Blue gradients interpolated in OKLab/OKLCH rather than CIELAB or sRGB
   Sources: [Ottosson, A perceptual color space for image processing (Oklab)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F); [Ottosson, Okhsv and Okhsl](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Fcolorpicker%2F); [CIE 1976 Lab\* (CIELAB)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FCIELAB_color_space).

### 4.2 Color Harmony Systems and Proportional Balance

Definition: Geometric angular relationships on the perceptual hue wheel ($h \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ)$ in OKLCH) paired with strict screen-area proportions that govern palette cohesion and focal tension.
Why:

- Emotion: Monochromatic and analogous chords induce calm, unity, and predictability; complementary and triadic chords induce tension, energy, or playful dynamism ([Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color)).
- Attention: Reserves the minority-area complement (`10%–15%` of screen area) exclusively for the primary focal anchor so the viewer's gaze locks immediately.
- Production value: Proportional area weighting (`60:30:10` or `85:15`) prevents the "circus/fruit-salad" look of equal-area high-chroma colors ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
- Clarity: Assigns distinct semantic roles (Background/Environment, Secondary Structure, Hero Accent) to each chord member.
- Cost: Algorithmic chord generation from a single seed hue `(L₀, C₀, h₀)` allows instant palette derivation across entire sequences.
  Rules:

1. Enforce exact angular offsets in OKLCH and strict **Screen-Area Proportions**:

| Harmony System             | OKLCH Hue Offsets ($\Delta h$)                                | Chroma ($C$) Constraints                                                    | Mandatory Screen-Area Ratio                                       | Target Emotional & Visual Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Monochromatic**          | $0^\circ$ ($\pm 5^\circ$)                                     | Vary $C \in [0.01, 0.22]$ and $L \in [0.12, 0.95]$                          | `60%` Dark/Light Base : `30%` Mid-tone : `10%` High-$L$ Highlight | Atmospheric unity, clinical restraint, noir or minimal elegance ([Itten](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color))                                                           |
| **Analogous**              | $h_0,\; h_0 \pm (25^\circ\text{–}40^\circ)$                   | Dominant $C \le 0.12$; Accent $C \in [0.14, 0.24]$                          | `60%` Primary : `30%` Adjacent : `10%` Outer Neighbor             | Naturalistic harmony, organic warmth or cool serenity ([Block](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)) |
| **Complementary**          | $h_0,\; h_0 + 180^\circ$ ($\pm 10^\circ$)                     | Background $C \le 0.08$; Hero Accent $C \ge 0.18$                           | `80%–85%` Dominant/Muted : `15%–20%` Hero Complement              | Maximum focal pop, conflict, cinematic teal-orange separation ([Itten](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color))                                                             |
| **Split-Complementary**    | $h_0,\; h_0 + 150^\circ,\; h_0 + 210^\circ$                   | Base $C \le 0.10$; One split muted ($C \le 0.12$), one vivid ($C \ge 0.18$) | `65%` Base : `25%` Secondary Split : `10%` Vivid Split            | High contrast with sophisticated variety; avoids binary harshness                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Triadic**                | $h_0,\; h_0 + 120^\circ,\; h_0 + 240^\circ$                   | Desaturate 2 hues ($C \le 0.08$), keep 1 hue vivid ($C \ge 0.18$)           | `70%` Muted Primary : `20%` Muted Secondary : `10%` Vivid Third   | Playful, illustrative, multi-character or categorical clarity                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Tetradic (Rectangular)** | $h_0,\; h_0 + 60^\circ,\; h_0 + 180^\circ,\; h_0 + 240^\circ$ | At least 2 hues must be near-neutral ($C \le 0.05$)                         | `55%` Base : `25%` Support : `12%` Sub-accent : `8%` Hero Accent  | Complex rich worlds; requires strict lightness hierarchy to avoid chaos                                                                                                                                                                           |

2. Never allow two high-chroma complementary hues ($C \ge 0.16$, $\Delta h \approx 180^\circ$) to occupy equal screen area (`50% : 50%`) or touch at equal lightness ($\Delta L < 0.10$), which triggers uncomfortable retinal border flutter ([Albers, Interaction of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbooks.google.com%2Fbooks%2Fabout%2FInteraction_of_Color.html%3Fid%3DA_MYU_XDXfcC)).
   Event mapping: Scene palette dictionary (`palette.base`, `palette.secondary`, `palette.accent`) referenced symbolically by material and planar element color channels; mood transitions execute via a synchronized `palette_lerp_event` across all bound elements.
   Raster/bake note: Pass palette colors as uniform buffer objects (UBOs) to fragment shaders so global harmony adjustments or scene-to-scene color transitions can execute dynamically without re-baking base albedo maps.
   Cultural notes: Hollywood blockbuster grading heavily relies on Complementary Teal-Orange ($h \approx 210^\circ\text{–}230^\circ$ shadows/backgrounds vs. $h \approx 35^\circ\text{–}55^\circ$ skin tones); Bauhaus and De Stijl (Mondrian, Rietveld) employ primary triads anchored by heavy black structural grids and high-key white negative space.
   Failure modes: Giving all three hues in a Triadic palette equal saturation ($C > 0.20$) and equal area (`33%` each) →garish, childish visual chaos where no focal point exists; placing pure saturated red text on a pure saturated cyan background at equal lightness → severe chromostereopsis and unreadable edge vibration.
   Checklist: [ ] Harmony system matches emotional objective [ ] Area distribution follows `60:30:10` or `85:15` rule [ ] At most 1–2 hues exceed high chroma ($C \ge 0.16$) per frame [ ] Zero equal-lightness ($\Delta L < 0.10$) touching complements
   Sources: [Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color); [Albers, Interaction of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbooks.google.com%2Fbooks%2Fabout%2FInteraction_of_Color.html%3Fid%3DA_MYU_XDXfcC); [Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152); [Ottosson, Oklab](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F).

### 4.3 Seven Contrasts of Color and Color-to-Emotion Mapping

Definition: Johannes Itten's seven fundamental mechanisms of color differentiation (Value/Light-Dark, Hue, Saturation/Quality, Temperature/Warm-Cool, Complementary, Simultaneous, and Extension/Proportion) and their quantitative mapping to viewer affective response ([Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color)).
Why:

- Emotion: Lightness (Value) and Chroma (Saturation) govern 70%+ of immediate emotional arousal and valence, even more strongly than hue alone `[OPINION]`.
- Attention: Value contrast ($\Delta L$) is processed fastest by the human magnocellular visual pathway, driving immediate pre-attentive eye fixation.
- Production value: Deliberate control of Simultaneous Contrast and Temperature Contrast creates luminous, painterly depth without relying on heavy geometry.
- Clarity: High value contrast guarantees silhouette and typographic legibility under any viewing condition.
- Cost: Adjusting lighting temperature and value contrast achieves dramatic mood shifts at zero geometric or simulation cost.
  Rules:

1. Prioritize **Value Contrast (Light-Dark)** above all other contrasts for focal legibility: maintain an OKLab lightness separation of $\Delta L \ge 0.35$ (or WCAG luminance contrast ratio $\ge 4.5:1$ for text and $\ge 3.0:1$ for key graphical subjects per [WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fcontrast-minimum.html) and [SC 1.4.11](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG21%2FUnderstanding%2Fnon-text-contrast.html)) between the hero subject and its immediate background.
2. Exploit **Warm-Cool (Temperature) Depth Contrast**: warm hues in OKLCH ($h \in [15^\circ, 85^\circ]$, red-orange-yellow) perceptually advance toward the viewer, while cool hues ($h \in [195^\circ, 275^\circ]$, cyan-blue) recede; assign warm accents to foreground focal elements and cool desaturated tones ($C \le 0.08$) to deep background planes ([Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color); [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
3. Compensate for **Simultaneous Contrast** (Albers effect): a neutral gray or muted element surrounded by a large dark field appears `10%–18%` lighter than the exact same color surrounded by a light field, and shifts in hue toward the complement of its background ([Albers, Interaction of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbooks.google.com%2Fbooks%2Fabout%2FInteraction_of_Color.html%3Fid%3DA_MYU_XDXfcC)). Offset the foreground swatch by $\Delta L = \pm 0.05$ and $\Delta h = 5^\circ\text{–}12^\circ$ toward the background hue when two elements across different backgrounds must appear identical to the viewer.
4. Apply the **Quantitative Color-to-Emotion Matrix**:

| Target Affective State             | OKLab Lightness ($L$)                       | OKLCH Chroma ($C$)                              | Dominant Hue Band ($h$)                                               | Value Contrast ($\Delta L$)                  | Psychological / Physiological Mechanism                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| :--------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Urgency / Danger / Aggression**  | Low–Mid ($0.20\text{–}0.55$)                | High ($0.20\text{–}0.30$)                       | Red–Orange ($20^\circ\text{–}45^\circ$)                               | Extreme ($\Delta L \ge 0.65$)                | High autonomic arousal; biological warning signal ([Itten](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color))                                                                             |
| **Joy / Optimism / Playfulness**   | High ($0.72\text{–}0.92$)                   | Mid–High ($0.14\text{–}0.24$)                   | Warm Yellow–Peach–Mint ($65^\circ\text{–}150^\circ$)                  | Moderate ($\Delta L \in [0.35, 0.55]$)       | High photopic luminance mimics daylight warmth                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Calm / Trust / Institutional**   | Mid–High ($0.45\text{–}0.85$)               | Low–Mid ($0.04\text{–}0.12$)                    | Cool Blue–Teal ($210^\circ\text{–}260^\circ$)                         | Soft–Moderate ($\Delta L \in [0.30, 0.50]$)  | Short-wavelength dominance lowers perceived visual clutter                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Melancholy / Isolation / Grief** | Low–Mid ($0.18\text{–}0.48$)                | Very Low ($0.01\text{–}0.06$)                   | Slate Blue–Cyan ($200^\circ\text{–}250^\circ$)                        | Compressed ($\Delta L \le 0.30$, except rim) | Chroma deprivation + compressed dynamic range mute affect ([Block](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)) |
| **Mystery / Uncanny / Cyberpunk**  | Low ($0.10\text{–}0.35$)                    | High Accents ($0.22\text{–}0.32$)               | Violet–Magenta–Acid Green ($300^\circ\text{–}350^\circ$, $135^\circ$) | High localized ($\Delta L \ge 0.60$)         | Non-naturalistic spectral combinations rare in daylight                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Luxury / Prestige / Gravity**    | Deep ($0.08\text{–}0.22$) or High ($0.90+$) | Achromatic ($0.00\text{–}0.03$) + Gold ($0.10$) | Neutral + Warm Amber ($70^\circ\text{–}85^\circ$)                     | Crisp ($\Delta L \ge 0.55$)                  | Extreme restraint in chroma signals exclusivity                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

Event mapping: Keyframed or event-driven shifts in `scene_contrast_profile` (`lightness_floor`, `lightness_ceiling`, `chroma_multiplier`, `temp_kelvin_offset`) triggered at narrative turning points.
Raster/bake note: Simultaneous contrast and warm-cool depth operate purely via pixel values; ensure baked lightmaps do not wash out local value contrast ($\Delta L \ge 0.35$) around the hero subject.
Cultural notes: White signifies purity and weddings in Western contexts but mourning and funerals in traditional East Asian and South Asian contexts; Red signifies danger/stop in Western industrial contexts but prosperity, celebration, and good fortune in Chinese and Vietnamese cultural contexts; Yellow/Gold signifies royalty and sacredness in East/Southeast Asia (`[OPINION]`; cross-cultural semiotics must be audited against the target region in §1.1).
Failure modes: Relying solely on Hue Contrast ($\Delta h = 120^\circ$) while leaving Lightness identical ($\Delta L = 0.0$) → image flattens completely into an indistinct mush for viewers with color-vision deficiency and fails the grayscale check; using high-chroma warm reds in the background and cool muted blues on the hero subject → background visually jumps in front of the subject.
Checklist: [ ] Hero subject has $\Delta L \ge 0.35$ against immediate background [ ] Foreground warm / background cool depth separation applied (or intentionally inverted with high $\Delta L$) [ ] Simultaneous contrast compensated when matching swatches across different backgrounds [ ] Cross-cultural color associations verified against target region
Sources: [Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color); [Albers, Interaction of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbooks.google.com%2Fbooks%2Fabout%2FInteraction_of_Color.html%3Fid%3DA_MYU_XDXfcC); [Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152); [WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2FWAI%2FWCAG22%2FUnderstanding%2Fcontrast-minimum.html).

### 4.4 Global Palettes, Per-Scene Palettes, and Color Scripts

Definition: A timeline-mapped visual score (color script) that plots the progression of dominant, secondary, and accent OKLCH swatches across every scene from `t = 0` to `t = end` to mirror the narrative and emotional arc.
Why:

- Emotion: Visualizes the macro-emotional journey at a glance (e.g., progressing from desaturated cold compression in Act I to warm high-contrast expansion in Act III) ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
- Attention: Uses inter-scene palette shifts as macro-pattern interrupts that refresh viewer attention at structural transitions.
- Production value: Prevents individual shots from looking like they were designed by unconnected artists in isolation.
- Clarity: Uses consistent color leitmotifs (assigning a reserved signature hue to a specific character, concept, or state) so the viewer tracks narrative threads effortlessly.
- Cost: Locking the color script before lighting and shader authoring avoids re-grading and re-baking scenes that clash when edited together.
  Rules:

1. Define a **Global Master Palette** of `5–7` core swatches (1 Dark Neutral $L \in [0.08, 0.16]$, 1 Light Neutral $L \in [0.90, 0.98]$, 1 Mid Neutral, 2 Primary Brand/World Hues, and 1–2 Reserved Hero Accents) that bounds the entire production `[OPINION]`.
2. Derive **Per-Scene Palettes** as constrained subsets (`3–4` active swatches per scene) of the Global Master Palette, shifting global lightness $L$, chroma $C$, and temperature bias across scenes to match the **Visual Intensity Curve** ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
3. Reserve the **Climax / CTA Swatch** (the highest-chroma or highest-contrast hue in the global palette, e.g., $C \ge 0.24$) so it appears at `< 2%` screen area in Acts I–II and expands to `15%–25%` screen area only at the narrative climax or Call to Action ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
4. Plot the **Color Script Strip** as a horizontal bar chart where bar width is proportional to scene duration ($t_{\text{scene}} / t_{\text{total}}$) and inspect adjacent scene boundaries: adjacent scenes within the same act should shift hue by $\Delta h \le 30^\circ$, whereas act boundaries or plot reversals should shift by $\Delta h \ge 90^\circ$ or $\Delta L \ge 0.30$ `[OPINION]`.
   Event mapping: Top-level `color_script_track` containing sequential `scene_palette_cue(start_time, duration, bg_oklch, mid_oklch, accent_oklch, ambient_light_kelvin)` events that drive scene uniforms.
   Raster/bake note: When baking static lightmaps for 3D scenes that undergo dynamic color-script shifts within the same shot, bake neutral white (`C = 0`) direct/indirect lightmaps into separate RGB channels (e.g., Red = Key, Green = Fill, Blue = Rim/Bounce) and tint them dynamically in the fragment shader via the `scene_palette_cue` uniform.
   Cultural notes: Feature animation studios (feature animation color-script workflows) popularized painted horizontal color scripts to govern lighting progression across 90-minute features; commercial motion studios use modular 8–12 frame digital color scripts for 30–60 second spots `[OPINION]`.
   Failure modes: Using the maximum-chroma climax color in Scene 1 at full saturation → visual nowhere left to go at the climax; changing palettes randomly every shot without narrative motivation → disjointed montage that confuses spatial/temporal continuity.
   Checklist: [ ] Global Master Palette bounded to `5–7` core swatches [ ] Per-scene palettes restricted to `3–4` active swatches [ ] Climax/CTA accent hue held back (`< 2%` area) until climax [ ] Color script strip proportional to scene durations verified
   Sources: [Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152); [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html).

### 4.5 Gradients, Interpolation Spaces, Banding Prevention, and Dithering

Definition: Continuous spatial transitions between two or more color stops (linear, radial, conic/angular, or 2D/3D freeform mesh gradients) evaluated in a perceptually uniform color space and quantized with noise dithering to eliminate Mach banding.
Why:

- Emotion: Smooth, luminous gradients evoke atmospheric depth, sky/horizon naturalism, and modern tactile polish.
- Attention: Soft vignette or directional gradients subtly steer gaze toward the brighter focal region without hard geometric borders.
- Production value: Visible 8-bit contour stair-stepping (Mach banding) and muddy gray midpoints in gradients are the single most common "cheap tell" in digital video production.
- Clarity: Preserves clean background fields behind typography without distracting contour rings.
- Cost: Fragment-shader OKLab interpolation + blue-noise dithering costs `< 0.1 ms` per frame at `4K` while saving massive bitrate and eliminating re-renders.
  Rules:

1. Never interpolate gradients between distant hues ($\Delta h > 60^\circ$) directly in display-encoded sRGB: sRGB nonlinear interpolation passes through a desaturated, darkened "muddy gray dead zone" at $t = 0.5$. Always interpolate color stops in **OKLab** (for smooth Cartesian transitions) or **OKLCH** (for vibrant chromatic arcs), or in **Scene-Linear RGB** when simulating physical light falloff ([Ottosson, Oklab](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F)).
2. Calculate the **Banding Risk Ratio**: when a gradient spans $D$ pixels across the screen with a total 8-bit digital code value delta of $\Delta V_{8\text{bit}}$ (where $V \in [0, 255]$), visible Mach banding is guaranteed whenever $\frac{D}{\Delta V_{8\text{bit}}} > 4.0\text{ pixels/step}$ (e.g., a subtle dark background gradient from `#141820` to `#202632` spanning `1920 px` has $\Delta V \approx 14$, giving $137\text{ px/step}$).
3. Eliminate Mach banding using two mandatory pipeline defenses:
   - Render and composite all gradients in `16-bit half-float` (`R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT`) or `10-bit/12-bit` integer buffers prior to final output encoding.
   - Apply **Triangular Probability Density Function (TPDF) Blue-Noise Dithering** with an amplitude of `±0.5 to ±1.0 LSB` (in target 8-bit/10-bit code values, i.e., `±1/255` for 8-bit or `±1/1023` for 10-bit) plus subtle monochromatic film grain (`0.8%–1.5%` RMS amplitude) before video compression so H.264/H.265 macroblock quantization does not re-introduce banding ([Vulkan Format Spec](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdocs.vulkan.org%2Fspec%2Flatest%2Findex.html); [DaVinci Resolve Manual](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdocuments.blackmagicdesign.com%2FUserManuals%2FDaVinci-Resolve-20-Beginners-Guide.pdf%3F_v%3D1757574013000)).
4. Space intermediate gradient stops along an eased curve (`cubic-in-out` or `sine-in-out`) rather than linear `0% -> 100%` stop clamping; linear stop boundaries have a discontinuous first derivative ($C^0$ only), which creates a perceived bright or dark crease at the start and end of the ramp (Mach band illusion) ([Albers, Interaction of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbooks.google.com%2Fbooks%2Fabout%2FInteraction_of_Color.html%3Fid%3DA_MYU_XDXfcC)).
   Event mapping: Planar shader uniform `gradient_descriptor(type="linear"|"radial"|"conic"|"mesh_patch", space="oklab"|"oklch"|"linear_rgb", stops=[(t, L, C, h)...], ease_curve="cubic_in_out", dither_lsb=1.0)`.
   Raster/bake note: Evaluate linear, radial, and conic gradients procedurally in the fragment shader in `fp16` precision with tiled `64×64` blue-noise texture lookup; for complex 2D mesh/patch gradients (Hermite/Coons patches), tessellate the patch with `≥ 32×32` subdivisions and interpolate vertex colors in OKLab in the fragment stage.
   Cultural notes: Vibrant multi-point mesh gradients ("aurora gradients" in OKLCH) define contemporary digital product branding, whereas subtle single-hue linear vignettes define classical narrative cinema grading.
   Failure modes: Rendering a dark vignette in an 8-bit buffer without dithering → concentric "onion-ring" contour bands pulse across the background during video playback; interpolating Blue (`#0044FF`) to Yellow (`#FFEE00`) in sRGB → a dull brownish-gray smear appears in the middle of the ramp.
   Checklist: [ ] Gradient interpolation space set to `oklab`, `oklch`, or `linear_rgb` (never raw sRGB for $\Delta h > 60^\circ$) [ ] Compositing buffer is `≥ 16-bit float` [ ] Blue-noise TPDF dithering (`±1.0 LSB`) applied before quantization [ ] Gradient stop falloff eased ($C^1$ continuous) to prevent Mach crease
   Sources: [Ottosson, Oklab](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbottosson.github.io%2Fposts%2Foklab%2F); [Albers, Interaction of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fbooks.google.com%2Fbooks%2Fabout%2FInteraction_of_Color.html%3Fid%3DA_MYU_XDXfcC); [Vulkan Documentation](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdocs.vulkan.org%2Fspec%2Flatest%2Findex.html).
