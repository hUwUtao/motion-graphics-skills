## 10. Materials & Shading

### 10.1 Physically Based Rendering (PBR) Metallic-Roughness Raster Approximation

Definition: An energy-conserving surface shading model parameterized by `Base Color (Albedo)`, `Metallic`, `Roughness`, `Normal`, `Specular IOR/F0`, and `Emissive` channels ([Burley, Physically Based Shading at Disney](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdisneyanimation.com%2Fpublications%2Fphysically-based-shading-at-disney%2F); [Khronos glTF 2.0 Specification](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc)).
Why:

- Emotion: Tactile surface fidelity—warm matte wood/fabric vs. cold brushed anodized aluminum or wet obsidian—triggers immediate sensory empathy and material trust.
- Attention: Controlled roughness variation (`Δroughness`) shapes the width and intensity of specular highlights, guiding the eye along hero bevels.
- Production value: Adhering to physical albedo bounds and roughness break-up eliminates the "1990s plastic CG Phong" look.
- Clarity: Distinct material families (dielectric vs. conductor, rough vs. polished) clearly separate mechanical components even when they share a monochromatic palette.
- Cost: The standard Cook-Torrance GGX + Schlick Fresnel + Split-Sum IBL metallic-roughness shader executes in a single raster pass on every modern GPU ([Khronos glTF 2.0 Spec](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc)).
  Rules:

1. **Strict Albedo Luminance Boundaries (No Pure Black or Pure White)**: In real-world physics, no diffuse surface absorbs 100% or reflects 100% of light. Clamp all dielectric `Base Color` values in linear RGB to:
   - **Darkest Dielectric Albedo (Coal / Fresh Asphalt / Black Velvet)**: Linear luminance $Y \ge 0.02\text{–}0.04$ (`30–50` in 8-bit sRGB, OKLab $L \ge 0.20$).
   - **Brightest Dielectric Albedo (Fresh Snow / Titanium White Paint)**: Linear luminance $Y \le 0.80\text{–}0.85$ (`235–242` in 8-bit sRGB, OKLab $L \le 0.94$) ([Burley, Physically Based Shading at Disney](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdisneyanimation.com%2Fpublications%2Fphysically-based-shading-at-disney%2F); [Khronos glTF 2.0 Spec](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc)).
     Setting albedo to `0.0` kills all indirect bounce light; setting albedo to `1.0` violates energy conservation and blows out highlights.
2. **Binary Metallic Discipline**: Set `Metallic` to either `0.0` (dielectrics: plastic, wood, skin, stone, glass, painted metal, with fixed Fresnel reflectance at normal incidence $F_0 = 0.04$ for $\text{IOR} = 1.5$) or `1.0` (raw unoxidized conductors: gold, silver, copper, aluminum, iron, where diffuse reflectance is `0.0` and `Base Color` tints $F_0 \in [0.55, 0.98]$) ([Khronos glTF 2.0 Spec](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc)). Use fractional metallic values (`0.1–0.9`) _only_ along narrow transition texels between dusty/rusted dielectric layers and bare metal.
3. **Mandatory Roughness Break-Up (Anti-CG-Uniformity Rule)**: Never assign a constant scalar `Roughness` (or `0.00` mirror roughness / `1.00` chalk roughness) to a hero surface. Clamp baseline roughness to `[0.04, 0.92]` and modulate it by `±0.05 to ±0.18` using a tiled micro-imperfection map (subtle smudges, brushed anisotropy, or Perlin noise) so specular highlights have organic texture rather than sterile mathematical edges `[OPINION]`.
4. **Bevel / Chamfer Mandate**: In nature, `0.0 mm` infinitely sharp $90^\circ$ geometric edges do not exist—they cannot catch a specular highlight unless a face points directly at the light. Apply a physical `1–3 segment` **Bevel / Chamfer** (`radius = 0.5–3.0 mm` for props, `2–8 mm` for furniture/architecture) or a baked **Rounded-Edge Normal Map** to every hard-surface mesh so edges catch continuous rim highlights ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
5. **Raster Approximations for Complex Dielectrics (Glass, Subsurface Scattering, Clearcoat)**:
   - **Refractive Glass / Liquid**: Approximate in the raster pipeline using **Screen-Space Refraction** (sampling the opaque scene color buffer offset by `view_normal.xy * (1.0 - 1.0/IOR) * thickness`, with chromatic dispersion offset `±0.003 UV` across R/G/B and roughness mip-level sampling) plus Schlick Fresnel environment reflection. **Quality loss vs. path tracing**: lacks multi-bounce internal total reflection and refractive caustics (fake caustics via a projected animated cookie texture on the floor light).
   - **Subsurface Scattering (Skin, Wax, Jade, Leaves)**: Approximate via **Separable Screen-Space Subsurface Scattering (SSSSS)** or a **Wrap-Lighting + Baked Thickness Map** shader (`back_light * exp(-thickness * extinction_rgb)` with red channel scattering `3×` farther than blue/green) ([Burley, Extending the Disney BRDF](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdisneyanimation.com%2Fpublications%2Fphysically-based-shading-at-disney%2F)).
     Event mapping: `material_pbr_descriptor(id, base_color_oklch, metallic=0.0|1.0, roughness_base=0.25, roughness_variance=0.08, ior=1.5, clearcoat=0.0, sss_thickness_map_id)` with optional dynamic events (`emissive_intensity_event` for glowing indicator cues).
     Raster/bake note: Enable **Specular Anti-Aliasing (Toksvig / Kaplanyan Normal Variance Mapping)** in the fragment shader (`roughness_aa = sqrt(roughness^2 + min(0.5, length(dFdx(N))^2 + length(dFdy(N))^2))`) so thin sharp bevels do not shimmer and sparkle with fireflies when the camera moves at a distance.
     Cultural notes: Contemporary hardware/industrial motion design ("tactile minimalism") contrasts ultra-matte bead-blasted surfaces (`roughness = 0.65`) directly against mirror-polished chamfered bevels (`roughness = 0.06`) on the exact same monochromatic object `[OPINION]`.
     Failure modes: Setting `Base Color = (0, 0, 0)` on a black product → looks like a flat black hole that ignores all bounce lighting; omitting bevels on a 3D cube → edges look razor-thin and alias severely; forgetting Specular AA on glossy metal meshes → crawling sub-pixel white fireflies across every moving frame.
     Checklist: [ ] Dielectric Base Color clamped to `[0.03, 0.85]` linear luminance [ ] Metallic set strictly to `0.0` or `1.0` (except dirt transitions) [ ] Roughness stays within `[0.04, 0.92]` with `±0.05–0.18` break-up variation [ ] All hard edges beveled (`1–3 segments`) or baked into normal maps [ ] Specular Anti-Aliasing (normal variance to roughness) active in fragment shader
     Sources: [Burley, Physically Based Shading at Disney](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdisneyanimation.com%2Fpublications%2Fphysically-based-shading-at-disney%2F); [Khronos glTF 2.0 Specification](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc); [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html).

### 10.2 Stylized and Non-Photorealistic Rendering (Toon, Hatching, Outlines, Matcaps) and Readability

Definition: Expressive, non-photorealistic raster shaders that quantize continuous light falloff into graphic bands (Cel/Toon shading), replace smooth gradients with procedural halftone/hatching strokes, or map surface normals directly to a 2D studio sphere texture (MatCap).
Why:

- Emotion: Stylized shaders bypass the "uncanny valley" of imperfect photorealism, evoking illustration, comic books, hand-crafted printmaking, or playful graphic clarity.
- Attention: Stepped luminance bands and silhouette ink outlines compress surface noise so the eye reads form and gesture instantaneously.
- Production value: Screen-space or distance-compensated outline thickness and art-directed shadow terminator hues (warm/cool chromatic shadow steps rather than multiplying by black) separate studio-grade NPR from cheap posterize filters.
- Clarity: Preserves graphic consistency when mixing 2D planar vector elements and 3D meshes in the same frame.
- Cost: MatCaps (`1` 2D texture fetch via view-space normal `N_view.xy * 0.5 + 0.5`) and quantized 2-band Cel shaders are the fastest 3D shaders possible (`< 0.1 ms` per frame, zero complex IBL math).
  Rules:

1. **Cel / Toon Quantization & Chromatic Shadow Rule**:
   - Compute diffuse lighting $d = N \cdot L \in [-1, 1]$ and quantize into `2` or `3` discrete graphic bands using a narrow anti-aliased `smoothstep(threshold - w, threshold + w, d)` where step width $w = 1.5 \times \text{fwidth}(d)$ (preventing jagged pixel stair-stepping along the shadow terminator).
   - **Never shade the shadow band by multiplying Albedo by black (`0.3 * albedo`)**: explicitly specify an independent **Shadow Swatch** in OKLCH that shifts lightness down by $\Delta L \in [-0.18, -0.30]$, increases or adjusts chroma by $\Delta C \in [+0.02, +0.06]$, and rotates hue by $\Delta h \in [15^\circ, 40^\circ]$ toward cool blue/violet (for warm key light) or warm violet/red (for cool key light) ([Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color)).
   - Proxy **Clean Shadow Terminators** on characters/faces by replacing noisy geometric vertex normals with a **Normalized Sphere/Ellipsoid Proxy Normal** (`N_proxy = normalize(pos_world - head_center_world)`) blended `60%–85%` over the true mesh normal so self-shadowing across cheeks and noses forms clean graphic shapes rather than jagged polygon triangles `[OPINION]`.
2. **Outline Rendering Architecture (Inverted Hull vs. Screen-Space Edge Detection)**:
   - Combine **Inverted Hull Extrusion** (rendering back-faces extruded along smoothed vertex normals by $w_{\text{px}} \cdot Z \cdot \tan(\text{FOV}/2) / H_{\text{px}}$ for outer silhouettes) with a **Screen-Space Normal + Linear-Depth Sobel/Roberts Cross Edge Pass** (catching interior creases where $|\nabla N| > 0.4$ or $|\nabla Z| / Z > 0.08$).
   - Scale outline thickness in **Screen Pixels (`1.5–4.0 px` at `1080p`)** multiplied by a weak depth dampener $(Z_{\text{ref}} / Z)^{0.35}$ and modulated by surface illumination $(1.2 - 0.5 \cdot \max(0, N \cdot L))$ so outlines thin out on bright lit edges and thicken (`1.4×`) in shadow creases—matching hand-inked calligraphy.
3. **Tonal Art Maps (TAMs) for Hatching & Halftones**:
   - When using cross-hatching or halftone dots in 3D, never scale a single static dot texture in world space (which turns into noisy moiré grain in the distance). Pre-build a **6-level Tonal Art Map (TAM) mip-chain** where zooming out keeps stroke thickness constant in screen pixels (`2–3 px` wide) while dropping out individual strokes, and sample via **Triplanar Object-Space Projection** blended with `50%` screen-space anchoring (`[OPINION]`, SIGGRAPH Praun et al. TAM technique).
4. **Delivery-Size Readability Audit (Spatial Frequency Ceiling)**:
   - Any surface texture detail, hatching line, or shader pattern whose period in screen space falls below the **Nyquist Limit of `2.5 pixels`** at delivery resolution will alias into shimmering moiré noise during camera motion. Automatically fade high-frequency procedural patterns to their mean color as screen-space derivative `max(length(dFdx(uv)), length(dFdy(uv)))` approaches `0.4`.
     Event mapping: `material_npr_descriptor(type="cel_2band"|"matcap"|"halftone_tam", lit_oklch, shadow_oklch, terminator_threshold=0.05, outline_px=2.5, proxy_normal_blend=0.75)`.
     Raster/bake note: All NPR techniques (Cel, MatCap, Inverted Hull, Sobel Depth/Normal Outlines, Triplanar TAMs) are 100% native to the GPU raster pipeline and require **zero ray tracing** and zero quality loss—in fact, raster pipelines offer superior explicit control over stylized shadow terminators and screen-space ink widths compared to physical path tracers.
     Cultural notes: Japanese anime studio CG (cel-shaded 3D anime pipelines) relies on custom vertex-color threshold weights and spherical proxy normals to mimic 2D _sakuga_ cel shading; Western _Spider-Verse_-inspired styles layer CMYK offset halftone dots (`15°`, `45°`, `75°` screen angles) inside shadows and Ben-Day dots inside highlights `[OPINION]`.
     Failure modes: Using un-smoothed hard-edge normals for an Inverted Hull outline → the extruded back-faces split apart at every hard corner, leaving gaping holes in the outline; applying world-space uniform outline thickness without depth scaling → distant background characters turn into solid black blobs.
     Checklist: [ ] Cel shadow band uses an explicit hue/chroma-shifted OKLCH swatch (never multiplied by black) [ ] Shadow step anti-aliased using `1.5 * fwidth(N·L)` [ ] Outlines combine Inverted Hull (silhouette) + Depth/Normal Sobel (creases) at `1.5–4.0 px` [ ] Outline thickness modulates with light direction (thinner on lit side, thicker in shadow) [ ] High-frequency patterns clamped above `2.5 px` period to prevent Nyquist moiré
     Sources: [Itten, The Art of Color](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.goodreads.com%2Fen%2Fbook%2Fshow%2F967310.The_Art_of_Color); [Blender Reference Manual (NPR / Line Art / EEVEE)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdocs.blender.org%2Fmanual%2Fen%2Flatest%2F).
