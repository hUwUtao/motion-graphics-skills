## 7. Animation Fundamentals

### 7.1 The 12 Principles in Declarative Event Systems

Definition: Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston's 12 foundational principles of animation ([The Illusion of Life](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.penguinrandomhouse.com%2Fbooks%2F727679%2Fillusion-of-life-the-by-frank-thomas%2F)) translated from hand-drawn keyframes into explicit, numeric, declarative event-channel rules.
Why:

- Emotion: Imbues geometric primitives and meshes with perceived mass, intention, vulnerability, and vitality.
- Attention: Anticipation cues prepare the viewer's eye for where motion is about to occur; Staging guarantees the action is never obscured.
- Production value: The presence of volume-preserving squash-and-stretch, anticipation, and follow-through immediately distinguishes crafted animation from mechanical linear interpolation.
- Clarity: Exaggeration and Solid Drawing/Posing make silhouettes and emotional states unmistakable at a glance.
- Cost: Declarative event templates (automatically pre-pending an anticipation event and appending an overshoot/settle event to any primary translation cue) deliver hand-crafted quality programmatically.
  Rules:

1. Translate each of the **12 Principles** into explicit numeric event parameters:

| Principle ([Thomas \& Johnston](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.penguinrandomhouse.com%2Fbooks%2F727679%2Fillusion-of-life-the-by-frank-thomas%2F)) | Declarative Event-Driven Rule & Numeric Range                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Squash and Stretch**                                                                                                                                             | Enforce constant volume/area: in 3D, $S_x \cdot S_y \cdot S_z = 1.0$ (if primary motion axis stretches to $S_y = 1.25$, set $S_x = S_z = 1/\sqrt{1.25} = 0.894$); in 2D, $S_x \cdot S_y = 1.0$. Realistic rigid/semi-rigid range: `±3%–8%`; stylized motion graphics: `±12%–25%`; slapstick cartoon: `±35%–60%`.                                                                                                         |
| **2. Anticipation**                                                                                                                                                   | Pre-pend a counter-directional event before any major primary motion: displacement = `8%–18%` of primary travel distance in the opposite direction (`-v_dir`), duration = `20%–35%` of primary action duration (`4–10 frames` @ `24 fps`), with a `1–2 frame` apex hold before the forward launch ([Williams](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)). |
| **3. Staging**                                                                                                                                                        | Only `1` primary kinetic event may peak within any `6–8 frame` (`250 ms`) window; maintain silhouette separation (`≥ 2% W` negative space gap around active limb/element) and luminance contrast (`ΔL ≥ 0.35`).                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **4. Straight Ahead vs. Pose-to-Pose**                                                                                                                                | Model **Pose-to-Pose** as discrete declarative state cues (`pose_event` at explicit timestamps `t₀, t₁, t₂`); model **Straight Ahead** as procedural/additive channel modifiers (noise, spring physics, or baked particle/fluid caches) layered on top of the pose sequence.                                                                                                                                             |
| **5. Follow-Through & Overlapping Action**                                                                                                                            | Append child/extremity phase lags (`Δt = 2–6 frames` per hierarchy depth level) and damped settle oscillations (`1–3 cycles`, amplitude `3%–12%` of travel) after the root element stops.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **6. Slow In and Slow Out**                                                                                                                                           | Ensure `≥ 90%` of non-continuous transform events use nonlinear easing (`cubic`, `expo`, `spring`), spending `60%–75%` of frames near the start/end poses and only `25%–40%` of frames in the high-velocity middle transit.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **7. Arcs**                                                                                                                                                           | Whenever an element translates diagonally across `(ΔX, ΔY)`, inject an orthogonal bow offset (`arc_bulge = 8%–20%` of chord length) or decouple X and Y easing curves (e.g., `X = cubic_out`, `Y = quad_in_out`) so the trajectory forms a smooth parabola rather than a rigid straight line.                                                                                                                            |
| **8. Secondary Action**                                                                                                                                               | Attach `1–2` subordinate supporting events (e.g., micro-tilt `3°–8°`, surface sheen pulse, or small companion particle puff) triggered at `t_primary + 2f` with screen area `≤ 20%` of the primary action; never let secondary action overpower the primary read.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **9. Timing**                                                                                                                                                         | Map frame counts @ `24 fps` to physical mass and intent: `2–4 frames` = instantaneous snap/shock; `6–10 frames` = crisp, light, energetic UI/graphic action; `14–24 frames` = standard natural weight; `30–60+ frames` = massive scale, heavy inertia, or solemn restraint ([Williams](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)).                        |
| **10. Exaggeration**                                                                                                                                                  | Push peak pose extremes (`scale`, `tilt`, `chroma`) by `+15%–35%` beyond physically literal values for `2–4 frames` around the motion apex so the kinetic energy survives motion blur and viewer blink intervals.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **11. Solid Drawing (3D Form)**                                                                                                                                       | Avoid parallel twin axes ("twinning"): offset left/right paired elements or limbs by `≥ 10%–20%` in angle and `2–4 frames` in timing; orient 3D meshes at `3/4` view angles (`25°–65°` yaw, `10°–25°` pitch) so three orthogonal planes remain visible.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **12. Appeal**                                                                                                                                                        | Enforce asymmetric proportion ratios (`1 : 1.618` or `60 : 30 : 10`) across character/graphic segments and pair complex detailed areas with resting smooth areas (detail-to-rest ratio `≤ 30 : 70`).                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

Event mapping: Composite macro-event `kinetic_move_event(target_id, from_pos, to_pos, start=t0, duration=16f)` that compiles into a 3-stage sequence on the element's channels: (1) `anticipation_cue(t0 - 5f, dur=5f, pos=from_pos - 0.12*delta, scale=[1.06, 0.94])`, (2) `transit_cue(t0, dur=16f, pos=to_pos, arc_offset=0.15, scale_peak=[0.88, 1.14])`, and (3) `settle_cue(t0 + 16f, dur=8f, spring_zeta=0.72)`.
Raster/bake note: Evaluate volume-preserving squash-and-stretch (`S_x * S_y * S_z = 1.0`) along the velocity tangent vector inside the vertex shader or transform hierarchy matrix (`R_vel * S_squash * R_vel^T`) so meshes deform along their direction of travel regardless of local pivot orientation.
Cultural notes: Western feature animation (classical feature animation) emphasizes continuous fluid arcs, overlap, and squash-and-stretch on `1s` (`24 fps`); Japanese _Limited Animation_ (stylized limited animation) intentionally holds extreme key poses for `6–18 frames` and transitions between them in `1–3 frames` with hand-drawn/mesh smear frames for explosive high-contrast impact `[OPINION]`.
Failure modes: Stretching an axis to `1.4×` without compressing the perpendicular axes to `0.714×` (2D) or `0.845×` (3D) → the object appears to inflate and grow in mass mid-flight; omitting anticipation on a fast `6-frame` launch → the viewer's eye only notices the object _after_ it has already arrived at its destination.
Checklist: [ ] Squash-and-stretch preserves volume ($S_x S_y S_z = 1.0$) [ ] Major launches preceded by `4–10 frame` (`8%–18%` distance) anticipation [ ] Only `1` primary action peaks per `6–8 frame` window [ ] Diagonal translations travel on curved arcs (`8%–20%` bow) [ ] Zero symmetrical "twinning" in paired limbs/elements
Sources: [Thomas \& Johnston, The Illusion of Life](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.penguinrandomhouse.com%2Fbooks%2F727679%2Fillusion-of-life-the-by-frank-thomas%2F); [Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit).

### 7.2 Timing vs. Spacing and Easing Families (Linear, Quad, Cubic, Expo, Back, Elastic, Bounce, Spring)

Definition: The separation of **Timing** (the exact frame timestamps `start` and `duration` when key events occur) from **Spacing** (how closely or widely intermediate positions are clustered between those timestamps via mathematical easing or spring functions) ([Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)).
Why:

- Emotion: Spacing defines the perceived personality and material physics of an element—from crisp, decisive intelligence (`expo-out`) to heavy industrial machinery (`cubic-in-out`) or playful buoyancy (`spring` / `elastic`).
- Attention: High initial acceleration (`ease-out`) creates an immediate velocity spike at `t = start`, capturing peripheral vision on frame 1 of the event.
- Production value: Using default symmetrical `ease-in-out` (`cubic-bezier(0.42, 0, 0.58, 1)`) on every single channel produces a floaty, robotic "underwater" feel that marks amateur work.
- Clarity: Rapid deceleration into a long settle ensures the element is nearly stationary and readable for the final `40%` of its event duration.
- Cost: Analytic easing curves and closed-form damped harmonic oscillators evaluate in nanoseconds per channel with zero baked curve tables.
  Rules:

1. Apply the **Canonical Easing & Spring Parameter Specification**:

| Easing Family            | Cubic-Bézier `(x1, y1, x2, y2)` or Physics Formula                                                               | Optimal Duration (`ms` / `frames @ 24 fps`) | Primary Use Case & Felt Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| :----------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Linear**               | `(0.00, 0.00, 1.00, 1.00)`                                                                                       | Any continuous window                       | Constant-rate mechanical rotation, UV scrolling, slow documentary camera creep, or linear-light cross-dissolves.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Quadratic (`Quad`)**   | Out: `(0.25, 0.46, 0.45, 0.94)`<br>In-Out: `(0.45, 0.03, 0.51, 0.95)`                                            | `150–300 ms` (`4–7 f`)                      | Gentle, low-energy micro-transitions (opacity fades, subtle color shifts, minor hover/highlight states).                                                                                                                                             |
| **Cubic**                | Out: `(0.22, 1.00, 0.36, 1.00)`<br>In: `(0.32, 0.00, 0.67, 0.00)`<br>In-Out: `(0.65, 0.00, 0.35, 1.00)`          | `250–500 ms` (`6–12 f`)                     | Workhorse standard for natural typography reveals, medium camera moves, and clean graphic layout transitions.                                                                                                                                        |
| **Exponential (`Expo`)** | Out: `(0.16, 1.00, 0.30, 1.00)`<br>In-Out: `(0.87, 0.00, 0.13, 1.00)`                                            | `400–800 ms` (`10–20 f`)                    | High-craft editorial snap: explosive initial velocity that spends the final `60%` of duration asymptotically settling into pixel-perfect alignment `[OPINION]`.                                                                                      |
| **Back (Overshoot)**     | Out: `(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1.00)`<br>(Overshoot param $s \in [1.1, 1.7]$)                                          | `300–550 ms` (`7–13 f`)                     | Single clean overshoot (`4%–10%` past target) for tactile card pop-ins, badge reveals, and energetic limb locks.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Damped Spring**        | $x(t) = 1 - e^{-\zeta \omega_n t}\left(\cos(\omega_d t) + \frac{\zeta}{\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}\sin(\omega_d t)\right)$ | Settle within `350–750 ms` (`8–18 f`)       | Physically grounded mass: use **Critically Damped** ($\zeta = 1.0$) for zero-overshoot luxury UI, **Slightly Underdamped** ($\zeta \in [0.68, 0.85]$, $\omega_n \in [14, 24]\text{ rad/s}$) for natural physical settle (`1–2` subtle oscillations). |
| **Elastic**              | Underdamped Spring with $\zeta \in [0.20, 0.45]$, $\omega_n \in [25, 45]\text{ rad/s}$                           | `500–900 ms` (`12–22 f`)                    | High-tension rubber band, string pluck, or cartoon boing (`3–5` visible oscillations); use sparingly (`≤ 1` element per scene) `[OPINION]`.                                                                                                          |
| **Bounce**               | Piecewise parabolic gravity arcs with coefficient of restitution $e \in [0.45, 0.68]$                            | `450–850 ms` (`11–20 f`)                    | Rigid collision against an unyielding floor/wall; each bounce duration scales by $e$ and height scales by $e^2$.                                                                                                                                     |

2. Enforce **Asymmetric In-Out Bias** on point-to-point moves: when an element moves from A to B inside the frame, make the acceleration phase faster (`30%–35%` of total time) and the deceleration phase longer (`65%–70%` of total time, e.g., `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 0.15, 1.0)`) so the motion feels propelled by an initial impulse rather than symmetrically drifting `[OPINION]`.
3. Never apply `Back`, `Elastic`, or `Bounce` easing to **Opacity** or **Lightness** channels—overshooting opacity above `1.0` or below `0.0` either clamps invisibly (wasting frames) or causes an unintended flash.
   Event mapping: Every `event` carries an explicit `easing` descriptor: either `easing: {type: "cubic_bezier", p: [0.16, 1.0, 0.3, 1.0]}` or `easing: {type: "spring", zeta: 0.75, omega_n: 18.0, v0: 0.0}`.
   Raster/bake note: Evaluate `cubic_bezier` via 4-iteration Newton-Raphson root-finding on $x(t)$ and evaluate `spring` via the exact analytical closed-form damped harmonic equation in the event runtime—never use Euler integration with variable frame steps, which causes spring amplitude to diverge across `24 fps` vs. `60 fps` renders.
   Cultural notes: High-fashion and architectural motion design relies almost exclusively on long, frictionless `Expo-Out` (`600–1000 ms`) and critically damped springs ($\zeta = 1.0$); mobile game and kids' broadcast motion relies heavily on `Back-Out` ($s = 1.5$) and `Elastic` ($\zeta = 0.35$) `[OPINION]`.
   Failure modes: Applying `Linear` easing to a camera pan or object slide → the start and stop frames look like the object hit an invisible brick wall; using `Expo-In-Out` with a short `6-frame` duration → the middle transit jumps `80%` of the screen width in a single frame, breaking visual tracking.
   Checklist: [ ] Entrances use `Ease-Out`; exits use `Ease-In`; in-frame moves use asymmetric `In-Out` or `Spring` [ ] Spring physics evaluated analytically ($\zeta \in [0.68, 1.0]$ for standard work) [ ] Zero `Back`/`Elastic`/`Bounce` curves applied to opacity channels [ ] `Expo` curves given sufficient duration (`≥ 10 frames` @ `24 fps`) so mid-transit does not teleport
   Sources: [Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit); [Lottie v1.0 Specification](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Flottie.github.io%2Flottie-spec%2F1.0%2F); [Khronos glTF 2.0 Animation Spec](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc).

### 7.3 Overlap, Follow-Through, Secondary Motion, Holds, Arcs, and Stagger/Offset

Definition: The temporal phase-shifting of channels across a single hierarchy (overlap/follow-through) or across an array of sibling elements (stagger/offset), combined with deliberate stationary holds and curved spatial trajectories ([Thomas \& Johnston, The Illusion of Life](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.penguinrandomhouse.com%2Fbooks%2F727679%2Fillusion-of-life-the-by-frank-thomas%2F); [Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)).
Why:

- Emotion: Breaks up rigid mechanical synchronicity so multi-part objects and ensembles feel alive, fluid, and choreographed.
- Attention: Staggered cascades guide the viewer's eye sequentially across a group rather than presenting a simultaneous wall of motion.
- Production value: Offsetting rotation by `2–4 frames` behind translation ("breaking joints") is one of the highest-leverage craft secrets in both 2D and 3D animation ([Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)).
- Clarity: Deliberate holds (`6–18 frames`) after high-velocity moves give the viewer's visual cortex time to register the new state.
- Cost: Algorithmic stagger functions (`start_i = t0 + i * Δt`) and hierarchical delay offsets generate rich complexity from a single base event definition.
  Rules:

1. **Channel Phase Offset (Single Element Overlap)**: Never start and end `Translation`, `Rotation`, and `Scale` on the exact same frame for an organic or expressive element. Offset `Rotation` by `+1 to +3 frames` after `Translation` starts, and allow `Rotation` settle to trail `+2 to +5 frames` after `Translation` finishes ([Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)).
2. **Hierarchical Chain Delay (Wave / Whip Principle)**: In a parent-to-child chain of $K$ segments (e.g., spine → shoulder → elbow → wrist, or card → header → icon → label), delay each child level $k \in \{1 \dots K\}$ by $\Delta t = 2\text{–}4\text{ frames}$ (`80–165 ms`) relative to its immediate parent, with angular amplitude decaying or amplifying by `0.75×–1.25×` per link depending on material stiffness ([Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit)).
3. **Ensemble Stagger Budgeting**: When staggering an array of $N$ sibling elements (e.g., grid cards, list items, kinetic words), cap the **Total Cascade Spread** ($(N - 1) \times \Delta t_{\text{stagger}}$) at `≤ 350–600 ms` (`8–15 frames` @ `24 fps`) regardless of how large $N$ is:
   $$\Delta t_{\text{stagger}} = \min\left(3\text{ frames},\; \frac{14\text{ frames}}{\max(1, N - 1)}\right)$$
   Apply an **Eased Stagger Distribution** (`quad_out` on the index offset $i/(N-1)$) so the first 2–3 items establish a clear rhythm and the remaining items cascade rapidly without making the viewer wait `3 seconds` for a 20-item grid to finish appearing `[OPINION]`.
4. **Moving Holds vs. Dead Holds**: In 3D and rich 2D scenes, replace "dead holds" (0.0 velocity across all channels, which makes the render look like a frozen still frame) with a **Moving Hold**—continue a micro-drift along the primary easing vector at `1%–3%` of peak velocity or apply a subtle breathing scale/drift (`±0.3%` scale over `48 frames`) during the reading window ([Thomas \& Johnston, The Illusion of Life](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.penguinrandomhouse.com%2Fbooks%2F727679%2Fillusion-of-life-the-by-frank-thomas%2F)). (Exception: Japanese anime / spider-verse stylized step-timing intentionally uses `0.0` velocity dead holds on `2s`/`3s` for graphic contrast).
   Event mapping:

- Hierarchy overlap: `channel_offset_rule(target_id, translate_delay=0f, rotate_delay=+2f, scale_delay=+1f, child_chain_lag=+3f)`.
- Ensemble stagger: `group_stagger_event(target_group_ids[], base_event, total_spread_max_frames=14, index_distribution="quad_out"|"radial_from_center")`.
  Raster/bake note: When rendering large staggered ensembles (`N > 100` instances), pass each instance's computed `start_time_offset` as a per-instance vertex attribute (`vec4 instance_timing`) and evaluate the easing curve in the vertex shader via hardware instancing (`vkCmdDrawIndexedInstanced`).
  Cultural notes: Classical Disney feature animation mandates Moving Holds to preserve the illusion of dimensionality; graphical motion design (contemporary design-driven motion studios) combines ultra-crisp `Expo-Out` primary motion with `2–3 frame` hierarchical secondary overlap on every sub-component `[OPINION]`.
  Failure modes: Using a fixed `4-frame` stagger on a 25-item grid → the cascade drags on for `100 frames` (`> 4 seconds`), boring the viewer to tears; stopping a 3D character or product mesh at `0.0` velocity across all axes for `3 seconds` → the object looks like a paused video screenshot ("dead hold").
  Checklist: [ ] Rotation/scale channels offset by `1–3 frames` from translation [ ] Child hierarchy links lag parents by `2–4 frames` [ ] Total ensemble stagger spread capped at `≤ 14–15 frames` (`≤ 600 ms`) [ ] 3D holds use `1%–3%` micro-drift Moving Holds (unless stylized stepped timing)
  Sources: [Thomas \& Johnston, The Illusion of Life](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.penguinrandomhouse.com%2Fbooks%2F727679%2Fillusion-of-life-the-by-frank-thomas%2F); [Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FThe_Animator%2527s_Survival_Kit).
