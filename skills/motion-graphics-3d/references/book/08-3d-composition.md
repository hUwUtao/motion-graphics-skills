## 8. 3D Composition

### 8.1 Spatial Layout, Staging, and Depth Cues (Overlap, Scale, Atmosphere, DoF, Parallax)

Definition: The organization of meshes and planar elements across Foreground (FG), Midground (MG), and Background (BG) Z-depth planes using five monocular depth cues to create compelling 3D dimensionality on a flat 2D projection ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
Why:

- Emotion: Deep-space staging with strong perspective convergence and parallax induces awe, scale, and immersion; flat-space orthographic staging induces detachment, graphic playfulness, or claustrophobia ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)).
- Attention: Combining sharp mid-ground focus with foreground/background depth-of-field blur and atmospheric haze funnels gaze directly onto the hero Z-plane.
- Production value: Layering at least three distinct Z-planes with foreground framing elements ("dirtying the frame") immediately elevates a 3D scene from a sterile asset viewer to cinema.
- Clarity: Clear silhouette overlap and atmospheric contrast compression prevent background clutter from merging with the foreground subject's edges.
- Cost: Using low-poly meshes or camera-facing planar cards in the far background with analytical exponential fog achieves vast perceived world scale at minimal vertex cost.
  Rules:

1. Divide every 3D scene along the camera's `-Z` view axis into three explicit depth zones with logarithmic distance ratios:
   - **Foreground (FG / "Dirty Frame" Layer)**: $Z_{\text{FG}} \in [0.25 \cdot Z_{\text{MG}},\; 0.60 \cdot Z_{\text{MG}}]$, occupying `10%–25%` of peripheral frame area (e.g., out-of-focus foliage, architectural pillar, floating particles) darkened by `-1.0 to -2.0 EV` relative to the midground to frame the subject ([Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
   - **Midground (MG / Hero Action Plane)**: $Z_{\text{MG}} = 1.0 \times$ focal distance, holding peak luminance contrast ($\Delta L \ge 0.45$), peak chroma, and zero defocus blur (`Circle of Confusion ≤ 1.0 px`).
   - **Background (BG / Environment Layer)**: $Z_{\text{BG}} \ge 2.5 \cdot Z_{\text{MG}}$ up to horizon, with local contrast compressed by `30%–70%` and chroma reduced by `25%–60%`.
2. Activate at least `3` of the **5 Monocular Depth Cues** in every deep-space shot ([Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152)):
   - **Contour Overlap (Interposition)**: Foreground elements partially occlude `5%–20%` of secondary midground/background shapes (avoiding edge-to-edge tangents).
   - **Relative Size / Perspective Convergence**: Place recognizable reference forms at $1\times$, $2\times$, and $4\times$ depth so perspective foreshortening ($\text{screen height} \propto 1/Z$) establishes world scale.
   - **Aerial / Atmospheric Perspective**: Apply exponential height/depth fog $T(d) = \exp(-\sigma_t \cdot d)$ with extinction coefficient $\sigma_t \in [0.005, 0.04]\text{ m}^{-1}$, shifting distant surfaces toward the sky/ambient scattering color ($L \in [0.55, 0.80]$, cool hue $h \in [205^\circ, 245^\circ]$, low chroma $C \le 0.06$) ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
   - **Depth of Field (Defocus)**: Apply physically parameterized Circle of Confusion (CoC) blur proportional to $|Z - Z_{\text{focus}}| / Z$.
   - **Motion Parallax**: Translate the camera laterally (`truck` or `orbit`) rather than purely rotating (`pan/tilt`) so foreground elements sweep across the screen `2.5×–6.0×` faster than background elements ($\omega_{\text{screen}} \propto v_{\text{cam}} / Z$).
     Event mapping: Scene layout hierarchy grouping elements into `layer_fg`, `layer_mg_hero`, and `layer_bg`, paired with `atmosphere_event(density_sigma, height_falloff, inscatter_oklch)` and `camera_dof_event(focus_distance_z, f_stop)`.
     Raster/bake note: Approximating volumetric atmospheric scattering without ray marching: evaluate analytical exponential height fog + directional Henyey-Greenstein phase function ($g \in [0.6, 0.85]$) per pixel in the depth-buffer post-pass, or use a froxel (frustum-aligned voxel) `160×90×64` compute grid. **Quality loss vs. path tracing**: analytical depth fog lacks volumetric multi-scattering and sharp god-ray occlusion shafts unless paired with a low-res screen-space radial occlusion or froxel shadow-map lookup.
     Cultural notes: Orson Welles and Gregg Toland (_Citizen Kane_) pioneered deep-focus (`f/8–f/16`) multi-plane staging where FG, MG, and BG remain simultaneously razor-sharp; modern commercial 3D motion design heavily favors shallow-DoF (`f/1.4–f/2.8`) macro-photography staging with creamy foreground bokeh `[OPINION]`.
     Failure modes: Placing a 3D mesh against an empty infinite plane with no FG or BG reference objects and a locked nodal pan → zero parallax and zero scale cues; allowing a background vertical edge to align exactly with the hero character's head contour ("false attachment tangent") → destroys depth separation.
     Checklist: [ ] Scene organized into explicit FG (`0.25–0.6× Z`), MG (`1.0× Z`), and BG (`≥ 2.5× Z`) planes [ ] At least 3 depth cues active (overlap, scale, atmosphere, DoF, parallax) [ ] Atmospheric perspective shifts distant Z toward lower contrast and cooler/desaturated OKLCH [ ] Camera moves include lateral translation (`truck`/`dolly`) to activate parallax [ ] Zero false-attachment tangents across Z-planes
     Sources: [Block, The Visual Story](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FThe-Visual-Story-Creating-the-Visual-Structure-of-Film-TV-and-Digital-Media%2FBlock%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9781138014152); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450); [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html).

### 8.2 Motivated Three-Point Lighting, High/Low-Key Ratios, and Sequencing

Definition: The directional, angular, and radiometric ratioing of Key, Fill, and Rim (Back) light sources—anchored to plausible environmental sources (motivated lighting)—to sculpt 3D form and establish mood ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
Why:

- Emotion: Key-to-Fill contrast ratio is the single strongest driver of dramatic tension: Low-Key (`8:1` to `16:1+`) evokes mystery, danger, and intimacy; High-Key (`1.5:1` to `2:1`) evokes optimism, safety, and clarity ([Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
- Attention: The brightest specular and diffuse wrap of the Key and Rim lights defines the exact surface planes the viewer's eye locks onto first.
- Production value: Separating the three visible planes of an object into distinct luminance steps (`100%` Key plane : `35%–50%` Fill plane : `12%–20%` Core Shadow + `120%–200%` Rim grazing highlight) creates tangible 3D solidity ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
- Clarity: A grazing Rim/Kicker light cuts the subject's dark shadow side clean away from dark backgrounds.
- Cost: In a GPU raster pipeline, limiting real-time shadow-casting dynamic lights to `1–2` per hero subject (Key + optional Rim) and folding Fill into spherical harmonics / ambient probes maximizes frame rate.
  Rules:

1. Configure **Three-Point Angular Geometry** relative to the camera-to-subject axis ($0^\circ$ azimuth, $0^\circ$ elevation):
   - **Key Light**: Azimuth `30°–45°` (Rembrandt/Loop lighting) or `60°–75°` (Side/Split dramatic lighting), Elevation `+25° to +45°` ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)).
   - **Fill Light**: Opposite side of camera at Azimuth `-35° to -60°`, Elevation `+5° to +20°`, with soft source angular diameter `≥ 3×` the Key light's angular diameter; **never enable hard shadows on a Fill light** (dual crossing shadow edges read as artificial stage Mistakes) ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
   - **Rim / Back / Hair Light**: Azimuth `135°–165°` behind the subject (opposite the Key), Elevation `+30° to +60°`, grazing the silhouette contour at `0.8×–2.5×` Key intensity.
2. Lock the **Key-to-Fill Ratio** ($\text{Key}+\text{Fill} : \text{Fill}$ in linear radiometric exposure stops) to the scene's genre and emotional target ([Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450)):
   - **High-Key (Comedy, Commercial Product, Kids, UI)**: `1.5:1` to `2:1` (`0.6–1.0 stop` difference), bright background (`L ∈ [0.65, 0.90]`).
   - **Naturalistic / Narrative Standard**: `3:1` to `4:1` (`1.5–2.0 stops` difference).
   - **Low-Key / Chiaroscuro (Thriller, Luxury Tech, Noir, Drama)**: `8:1` to `16:1+` (`3.0–4.0+ stops` difference), dark background (`L ∈ [0.05, 0.22]`) with `60%–80%` of frame pixels below middle gray (`0.18` linear).
3. Enforce **Color Temperature Opposition** between Key and Fill: if the Key light is warm daylight/tungsten (`3200K–5200K`, $h \approx 55^\circ\text{–}75^\circ$), tint the Fill light toward cool sky/shadow (`6500K–9000K`, $h \approx 220^\circ\text{–}250^\circ$, $C \in [0.03, 0.08]$), or vice versa ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
4. **Sequential Light Reveal Choreography**: For hero product or title reveals in dark low-key environments, stagger light ignition events rather than turning all lights on at once: (1) Rim Light sweeps across silhouette (`t = 0`, `duration = 18–30f`) → (2) Fill / Environment Probe fades up (`t = +12f`, `duration = 20f`) → (3) Key Light + Specular sweep ignites (`t = +20f`, `duration = 24f`, `cubic-out`) `[OPINION]`.
   Event mapping: `light_rig_event(subject_id, key_azimuth=40, key_elev=35, key_intensity=4.0, key_kelvin=4800, key_to_fill_ratio=4.0, fill_kelvin=7500, rim_azimuth=150, rim_intensity=5.0)`.
   Raster/bake note: In a GPU rasterizer without ray-traced area lights, approximate soft area-light penumbras using **Percentage-Closer Soft Shadows (PCSS)** with `16–32` Poisson-disk blocker/PCF samples and Linearly Transformed Cosines (LTC) for polygonal area-light specular reflections. **Quality loss vs. path tracing**: PCSS can exhibit banding or contact-hardening artifacts at extreme penumbra widths (`> 15%` shadow map UV span), which must be masked with temporal blue-noise jitter.
   Cultural notes: Classical Hollywood studio portraiture (Paramount/Butterfly and Rembrandt lighting) established the `45°` Key triangle on the shadow cheek; contemporary luxury hardware commercials (smartphones, watches) use animated rectangular LTC area-light cards ("light-sweep rigs") that glide across curved bevels to trace form via specular reflections rather than diffuse lighting `[OPINION]`.
   Failure modes: Placing the Key light at `0°` azimuth/elevation directly on the camera axis ("flat frontal flash") → destroys all surface normal gradients ($N \cdot L \approx 1.0$ everywhere) and makes a 3D mesh look like a flat 2D cutout; casting two distinct hard shadows from Key and Fill → looks like a cheap multi-bulb stage set.
   Checklist: [ ] Key light positioned at `30°–75°` azimuth, `+25°–45°` elevation [ ] Key-to-Fill ratio explicitly set (`2:1` high-key, `4:1` natural, `8:1+` low-key) [ ] Fill light has zero hard shadow casting and opposite color temperature to Key [ ] Rim light separates dark subject edges from dark backgrounds [ ] PCSS + LTC area specular used for soft raster light sources
   Sources: [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html); [Brown, Cinematography: Theory and Practice](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.routledge.com%2FCinematography-Theory-and-Practice-For-Cinematographers-and-Directors%2FBrown%2Fp%2Fbook%2F9780367373450).

### 8.3 Baked Global Illumination, Lightmaps, Light Probes, and Ambient Occlusion

Definition: The offline precomputation of indirect bounce lighting, sky irradiance, glossy environment reflections, and crease shadowing into static UV lightmaps, Spherical Harmonics (SH) / tetrahedral light probe volumes, cubemap reflection probes, and Ambient Occlusion (AO) textures.
Why:

- Emotion: Indirect bounce light and color bleeding unify objects with the ground plane and environment, creating warmth, realism, and tactile presence ([Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html)).
- Attention: Contact Ambient Occlusion anchors objects physically to surfaces so the viewer's eye accepts the spatial illusion without distraction.
- Production value: Missing contact shadows and un-occluded glowing crevices ("floating object syndrome") are the #1 giveaway of un-baked, low-budget raster 3D.
- Clarity: Preserves surface form definition inside direct-light shadow regions via directional irradiance rather than flat constant ambient terms.
- Cost: Offline light baking shifts 100% of multi-bounce global illumination cost to pre-production asset compilation, enabling path-traced-quality indirect lighting in a `< 2 ms` GPU raster pass.
  Rules:

1. **Static Geometry → Directional UV Lightmaps**:
   - Generate a dedicated, non-overlapping **Second UV Channel (`UV1`)** for all static architecture and environment meshes with a minimum inter-island gutter padding of `≥ 4 texels` at target lightmap resolution (`e.g., 4/1024 = 0.0039 UV units`) to prevent bilinear mipmap light bleeding across seams ([Blender Reference Manual](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdocs.blender.org%2Fmanual%2Fen%2Flatest%2F)).
   - Set **Lightmap Texel Density** to `16–32 texels per world meter` for hero midground surfaces, `8–12 texels/meter` for secondary surfaces, and `2–4 texels/meter` for distant background geometry `[OPINION]`.
   - Bake **Directional Lightmaps** (storing both irradiance RGB and dominant incoming light direction via Spherical Harmonics L1 or Half-Life 2 basis vectors) rather than flat scalar lightmaps, so normal maps still receive directional bump shading inside indirect shadow zones.
2. **Dynamic / Moving Meshes → Light Probes & Reflection Probes**:
   - Sample indirect diffuse irradiance on moving meshes from a 3D tetrahedral or grid **Light Probe Volume** encoded as **Order-2 (L2) Spherical Harmonics** (`9 coefficients × 3 RGB channels = 27 floats per probe`), spaced every `1.0–2.5 meters` near hero paths.
   - Sample indirect specular reflections from parallax-corrected (box-projected or sphere-projected) **Pre-convolved Cubemap Reflection Probes** (`256×256` to `512×512` per face in `BC6H` / `fp16`, mip-chain pre-filtered for GGX roughness $\alpha \in [0.0, 1.0]$ via split-sum approximation).
3. **Dual-Layer Ambient Occlusion (Baked + Screen-Space)**:
   - Bake micro-cavity and local object self-occlusion (`radius = 0.05–0.30 m`) into the mesh's `AO texture channel` (or vertex color alpha).
   - Layer real-time **Ground-Truth Ambient Occlusion (GTAO)** or **HBAO+** in screen space (`radius = 0.25–1.0 m`, intensity `0.4–0.7`) to supply dynamic contact shadows where moving objects approach static floors.
   - **Physical Shading Rule**: Apply Ambient Occlusion _only_ to **Indirect Diffuse and Indirect Specular (Specular Occlusion)** terms—never multiply AO over Direct Key/Rim light (`Direct_Light * (1.0 - AO)` is physically wrong and creates dirty black soot inside sunlit creases) ([Burley, Physically Based Shading at Disney](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdisneyanimation.com%2Fpublications%2Fphysically-based-shading-at-disney%2F); [Khronos glTF 2.0 Spec](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc)).
     Event mapping: Scene bake manifest `bake_lighting_config(lightmap_texels_per_m=24, directional_sh="L1", probe_grid_spacing_m=1.5, reflection_cubemap_res=512, gtao_radius_m=0.5, gtao_intensity=0.55)`.
     Raster/bake note: **Quality loss vs. runtime path tracing**: Baked lightmaps cannot update when large walls move, and cubemap reflection probes cannot show accurate self-reflections or dynamic moving character reflections on curved mirrors. Mitigate on flat floors/water using a **Planar Reflection Render Pass** (rendering the scene mirrored across the floor plane at `50%` resolution) or **Screen-Space Reflections (SSR)** blended seamlessly into the baked cubemap fallback at screen edges.
     Cultural notes: Stylized "clay-render" and miniature diorama motion graphics rely heavily on exaggerated multi-bounce GI and soft contact AO (`radius = 0.5–1.5 m`) to make digital meshes feel like physical tabletop studio models built from matte paper, wood, or vinyl `[OPINION]`.
     Failure modes: Multiplying AO directly on top of the final rendered pixel or direct sunlight → creases stay pitch black even when a spotlight shines directly into them; setting lightmap island padding `< 2 texels` → dark black seams appear along every geometric edge when the camera pulls back and lower mip levels activate.
     Checklist: [ ] Non-overlapping `UV1` lightmap layout with `≥ 4 texel` island padding [ ] Directional (L1 SH) lightmaps baked at `16–32 texels/m` on hero static surfaces [ ] L2 SH Light Probes + Parallax-Corrected Cubemap Probes placed for moving objects [ ] AO applied strictly to indirect lighting (never multiplied over direct light) [ ] Planar reflection or SSR + cubemap fallback configured for hero reflective surfaces
     Sources: [Birn, Digital Lighting and Rendering](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2F3drender.com%2Flight%2Findex.html); [Khronos glTF 2.0 Specification](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FKhronosGroup%2FglTF%2Fblob%2Fmain%2Fspecification%2F2.0%2FSpecification.adoc); [Blender Reference Manual](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdocs.blender.org%2Fmanual%2Fen%2Flatest%2F); [Burley, Physically Based Shading at Disney](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdisneyanimation.com%2Fpublications%2Fphysically-based-shading-at-disney%2F).
